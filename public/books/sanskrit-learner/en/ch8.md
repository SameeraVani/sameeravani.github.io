# Chapter 8: Verb Conjugations (धातु-रूपाणि)

Sanskrit verb roots are called **Dhatus**. Verbs conjugate based on tenses/moods (Lakaras), person (Purusha), and numbers.

---





---

## 1. Grammar Introduction

### 10 Lakaras (Tenses & Moods)
In Sanskrit, ten Lakaras (tenses and moods) are used to indicate time and mood. As stated in grammar texts:

> **लट् वर्तमाने लेट् वेदे भूते लङ् लुङ् लिटस्तथा ।**
> **विध्याशिषोस्तु लिङ् लोटौ लुट् लृट् लृङ् च भविष्यति ॥**

#### Details of the 10 Lakaras:
1. **लट् लकारः (Lat):** वर्तमानकाल-अर्थे (Present Tense)
2. **लेट् लकारः (Let):** वेदमात्रगोचरः (Subjunctive mood - used only in the Vedas)
3. **लङ् लकारः (Lang):** अनद्यतनभूतकाल-अर्थे (Imperfect Past Tense - past of yesterday/earlier)
4. **लुङ् लकारः (Lung):** सामान्यभूतकाल-अर्थे (Aorist Past Tense - general past)
5. **लिट् लकारः (Lit):** परोक्षभूतकाल-अर्थे (Perfect/Remote Past Tense - which the speaker did not witness)
6. **विधिर्लिङ् लकारः (Vidhi-Ling):** विध्यर्थे (Potential / possibility / duty / request)
7. **आशीर्लिङ् लकारः (Aashi-Ling):** आशीर्वाद-अर्थे (Benedictive / blessings)
8. **लोट् लकारः (Lot):** आज्ञा-प्रार्थना-आशीर्वाद-अर्थे (Imperative mood / command & request)
9. **लूट् लकारः (Lut):** अनद्यतनभविष्यत्काल-अर्थे (First Future Tense / Periphrastic Future)
10. **लृट् लकारः (Lrut):** सामान्यभविष्यत्काल-अर्थे (Second Future Tense / Simple Future)
11. **लृङ् लकारः (Lrung):** सङ्केतकाल-अर्थे (Conditional Tense / Conditional Future)

*In this book, we study the six main tenses/moods (Lat, Lang, Lit, Lrut, Lot, Vidhi-Ling).*

### Person (पुरुषः - Purusha)
Sanskrit verbs have three persons (Purushas):
* **Uttamapurusha (First Person):** Used when the subject is 'Asmad' pronouns (I, we two, we all).
* **Madhyamapurusha (Second Person):** Used when the subject is 'Yushmad' pronouns (you, you two, you all).
* **Prathamapurusha (Third Person):** Used when the subject is any other noun or pronoun (he, she, it, Rama, etc.).

### Number (वचनम् - Vachana)
Vachana refers to grammatical number. Sanskrit has three numbers:
* **Ekavachanam (Singular):** Used for one object or person (e.g., pathati - he reads).
* **Dvivachanam (Dual):** Used for two objects or people (e.g., pathatah - they two read).
* **Bahuvachanam (Plural):** Used for three or more objects or people (e.g., pathanti - they all read).

### Verb Root Divisions (धातुभेदाः)
Sanskrit verb roots are classified into three types:
1. **Parasmaipadina:** Roots whose action is intended for others (e.g. pathati - he reads).
2. **Atmanepadina:** Roots whose action is intended for oneself (e.g. vandate - he bows).
3. **Ubhayapadina:** Roots that can conjugate in both Parasmaipada and Atmanepada (e.g. karoti / kurute - to do).
