# The Golden Age & The Eloi

In another moment we were standing together in a little open space on the brow of the hill. I looked at the Eloi—at these exquisite little creatures who had welcomed me. They were a peaceful, delicate people, dressed in soft tunic robes and sandals, their hair curly and faces bright.

"What could have happened to humanity?" I thought. "Have we achieved the perfect state where struggle is no longer necessary?"

## The Garden of the Eloi

I was in the year **Eight Hundred and Two Thousand, Seven Hundred and One** A.D. The air was warm and sweet, filled with the scent of brilliant flowers that I had never seen before. All around me were ruins of great, majestic buildings—not ruins of decay, but like beautiful palaces that had been abandoned.

Here are my initial observations of this future society:

1.  **No Sign of Labor:** There were no shops, no factories, no signs of agriculture or industry.
2.  **Fruitarian Diet:** The Eloi fed exclusively on fruit, which grew in abundance on trees that seemed to be self-sustaining.
3.  **No Language Barriers:** They spoke a simple, musical language of very few words.
4.  **No Social Hierarchy:** There were no signs of government, class, or individual ownership.

> "It seemed to me that I had happened upon humanity at its golden summit. The red slopes of the hills were covered with a thick growth of sweet-smelling shrubs, and the valley was a garden of wonders."

But as the day wore on, I noticed something else. The Eloi were small, frail, and lacked any depth of intellect. They were like children, laughing and playing, but easily fatigued and completely devoid of curiosity. They seemed to live in a state of perpetual ease, yet under the surface, there was a strange, silent terror that crept in whenever the sun began to set.
