## Śrīmannyāyasudhā (Śrīmannyāyasudhā)

**The commentary written by Śrīmad Jayatīrtha Muni on Śrīmad Brahmasūtrānuvyākhyāna, composed by Śrīmad Ānandatīrtha Bhagavatpādācārya**  
**Śrīmannyāyasudhā**

oṃ || śriyaḥ patye nityāgaṇitaguṇamāṇikyaviśadaprabhājālollāsopahatasakalāvadyatamase |  
jagajjanmasthemapralayaracanāśīlavapuṣe namo'śeṣāmnāyasmṛtihṛdaydīptāya haraye || 1 ||

**Sameeravani**  
This work is a commentary on the work named 'Anuvyakhyana' composed by Srimad Madhvacharya, and its authorship is by Srimad Jayatirtha Muni. The name of this work is 'Srimannyayasudha'.

* **श्रियः पत्ये** (śriyaḥ patye) – To the consort of Goddess Mahalakshmi.
* **नित्यागणितगुणमाणिक्यविशदप्रभाजालोल्लासोपहतसकलावद्यतमसे** (nityāgaṇitaguṇamāṇikyaviśadaprabhājālollāsopahatasakalāvadyatamase) – To Him who has permanently destroyed the darkness of all defects by the effulgent light emanating from the rubies of His eternal, countless auspicious attributes.
* **जगज्जन्मस्थेमप्रलयरचनाशीलवपुषे** (jagajjanmasthemapralayaracanāśīlavapuṣe) – To Him whose auspicious form naturally performs the creation, sustenance, dissolution, etc., of the universe.
* **अशेषाम्नायस्मृतिहृदयदीप्ताय** (aśeṣāmnāyasmṛtihṛdayadīptāya) – To Him who is revealed as the core import of all the eternal Vedas (Apurusheya) and auxiliary texts/Smritis (Purusheya).
* **हरये** (haraye) – To Sri Hari, the destroyer of all sins.
* **नमः** (namaḥ) – Salutations.

**Bhava-Artha (Overall Meaning):** May my salutations be to Sri Hari, the consort of Sri Mahalakshmi, who destroys the darkness of all flaws with the divine luster of His eternal and countless auspicious attributes, whose divine form naturally creates, sustains, and dissolves the universe, who is the ultimate import of all the Vedas, Smritis, and Puranas, and who is the remover of all sins of His devotees.

**Nyayasudha**  
yena prādurabhāvi bhūmivalaye vyastāri gosantatiḥ prābodhi śrutipaṅkajaṃ karuṇayā prākāśi tattvaṃ param |  
dhvāntaṃ dhvaṃsamānāyi sādhunikaraś cākāri sanmārgagas tena vyāsadivākareṇa satataṃ mā tyāji me mānasam || 2 ||

**Sameeravani**

* **येन** (yena) – By whom, i.e., by the sun in the form of Sri Vedavyasa.
* **भूमिवलये** (bhūmivalaye) – On the earth.
* **प्रादुर्भावि** (prādurabhāvi) – Incarnated/manifested.
* **गोसन्ततिः** (gosantatiḥ) – The collection of Vedic literature (as well as rays of light).
* **व्यस्तारि** (vyastāri) – Expanded (divided into four parts).
* **श्रुतिपङ्कजं** (śrutipaṅkajaṃ) – The lotus in the form of the Vedas.
* **प्राबोधि** (prābodhi) – Awakened/blossomed.
* **करुणया** (karuṇayā) – Out of compassion.
* **परम् तत्त्वम्** (param tattvam) – The supreme absolute truth (Sri Hari).
* **प्राकाशि** (prākāśi) – Manifested/revealed.
* **ध्वान्तं** (dhvāntaṃ) – The darkness of ignorance.
* **ध्वंसम् अनायि** (dhvaṃsam anāyi) – Led to destruction.
* **साधुनिकरः च** (sādhunikaraś ca) – And the collection of virtuous people.
* **सन्मार्गगः** (sanmārgagaḥ) – Traveler on the right path (the path of liberation).
* **आकारि** (ākāri) – Made.
* **तेन** (tena) – By such a one.
* **व्यासदिवाकरेण** (vyāsadivākareṇa) – By the sun in the form of Sri Vedavyasa.
* **मे मानसम्** (me mānasam) – My mind.
* **सततम्** (satatam) – Constantly.
* **मा त्याजि** (mā tyāji) – May not be abandoned (may He always reside in my mind).

**Bhava-Artha:** May my mind never be abandoned by the all-powerful sun in the form of Sri Vedavyasa, who incarnated on this earth and, out of deep compassion, expanded the Vedic literature, caused the lotus of the Vedas to blossom, revealed the supreme truth (Sri Hari), destroyed the darkness of ignorance, and guided the virtuous onto the path of liberation. May He always shine within my heart.

**Nyayasudha**  
vyāptir yasya nije nijena mahasā pakṣe sapakṣe sthitir vyāvṛttiś ca vipakṣato'tha viṣaye saktir na vai bādhite |  
naivāsti pratipakṣayuktir atulaṃ śuddhaṃ pramāṇaṃ sa me bhūyāt tattvavinirṇayāya bhagavān ānandatīrtho muniḥ || 3 ||

**Sameeravani**

This verse praises Śrīmad Ānandatīrtha Bhagavatpāda (Śrīmad Madhvācārya). A highly clever use of **Śleṣa Alaṅkāra** (double entendre/pun) is employed here. On one hand, this verse defines the characteristics of a flawless **'Anvaya-Vyatireki'** logical inference (Anumāna Pramāṇa), and on the other hand, the exact same characteristics apply to the venerable Acārya.

**1. Primary Meanings (The Dual Interpretation)**

The *Parimala* commentary clarifies that this verse applies equally to both interpretations:
* **From the perspective of Pramāṇa (Logical Proof):** May that incomparable, pure, and flawless *Anvaya-Vyatireki* logical inference be the source of my determination of truth (valid knowledge).
* **From the perspective of the Acārya (Śrī Madhvācārya):** May that incomparable, pure, and faultless embodiment of pramāṇa, Sage Anandatirtha (Lord Madhvacarya), bless me with the determination of truth (knowledge of Brahman).

**2. Explanation of the Five Aspects according to Parimala**

The *Parimala* commentary explicitly lists the five aspects of a valid, flawless inference: **"pakṣavṛttitvaṃ sapakṣe sattvaṃ vipakṣādvyāvṛttirabādhitaviṣayatvaṃ asatpratipakṣatvaṃ ceti anvayavyatirekiṇaḥ pañcarūpāṇi..."** (Being present in the subject, presence in similar instances, absence from contrary instances, having an uncontradicted subject, and lack of a counter-argument). The way these five characteristics map onto Śrī Madhvācārya is described below:

* **I. Pakṣe sthitiḥ (Presence in the Subject / Pakṣavṛttitvam)**
  * **In Inference:** The indicator (hetu) must reside in the subject (pakṣa).
  * **In the Acārya:** According to *Parimala*, 'pakṣa' refers to **"nije bhaktavargāsye"**, i.e., in the mouths and minds of his devotees. When doubt arises in the minds of the devotees regarding the supremacy of Lord Vishnu, the Acārya pervades (**vyāpnoti**) their minds with his divine knowledge and power (**nijena mahasā**) and stabilizes the knowledge of Vishnu's supremacy. This is the Acārya's 'presence in the subject'.

* **II. Sapakṣe sthitiḥ (Presence in Similar Instances / Sapakṣe Sattvam)**
  * **In Inference:** The indicator (hetu) must also be present in similar cases (sapakṣa).
  * **In the Acārya:** Here, 'sapakṣa' refers to **'Hari' (Lord Vishnu)**, who is always free from doubt and possesses absolute truth. Śrī Madhvācārya's devotion and stance are always firmly fixed upon Lord Hari (**"hariviṣaye kṛtsnāviṣyatvāt"**).

* **III. Vyāvṛttiś ca vipakṣato (Absence from Contrary Instances / Vipakṣād Vyāvṛttiḥ)**
  * **In Inference:** The indicator must be completely absent from contrary instances (vipakṣa).
  * **In the Acārya:** Here, 'vipakṣa' refers to **unworthy/unqualified people** who are devoid of the knowledge of the absolute truth. Śrī Madhvācārya stays far away from such false scholars and sophists and does not teach them his secret doctrines (**"upadeśādāvapravṛttiḥ"**).

* **IV. Viṣaye saktiḥ na vai bādhite (Having an Uncontradicted Subject / Abādhitaviṣayatvam)**
  * **In Inference:** The subject matter must not be contradicted by a stronger pramāṇa (like direct perception).
  * **In the Acārya:** *Parimala* states that the word **'vai'** is used for emphasis. The Acārya never has attachment (sakti) towards contradicted subjects (i.e., false or forbidden things). He is completely free from delusion, as the pure knowledge of a soul of the 'Riju' class is by nature dedicated to truth (**"ṛjujñānasya yathārthaikasvabhāvatvāt"**).

* **V. Naivāsti pratipakṣayuktiḥ (Lack of Counter-Argument / Asatpratipakṣatvam)**
  * **In Inference:** There must be no equivalent counter-argument (pratipakṣa) that proves the opposite.
  * **In the Acārya:** This means that Śrī Madhvācārya has no equal opponent. As clarified in *Parimala*— **"pratibhaṭaśūnya ityarthaḥ"** (devoid of a rival). No opponent is capable of presenting an argument strong enough to refute the Acārya's doctrine.

**3. Conclusion**
* **Atulaṃ śuddhaṃ pramāṇaṃ:** He is incomparable (**atulam**) and free from all defects (**śuddham**), a veritable embodiment of pramāṇa.
* **Tattvavinirṇayāya bhagavānānandatīrtho muniḥ sa me bhūyāt:** Just as the result of a pure inference is valid knowledge of reality, may the divine Sage Anandatirtha destroy my ignorance and doubt and grant me the determination of the absolute truth.

The end of *Parimala* also notes that some thinkers identify the Vedas, the Mahabharata, and Buddhist/heterodox texts as the *pakṣa*, *sapakṣa*, and *vipakṣa* respectively (**"vedabhāratabauddhādigranthāḥ pakṣasapakṣavipakṣā ityeke"**), which highlights the philosophical depth of this verse.

bhavati yadanubhāvād eḍamūko'pi vāgmī jaḍamatir api jantur jāyate prājñamauliḥ |  
sakalavacanacetodevatā bhāratī sā mama vacasi nidhattāṃ sannidhiṃ mānase ca || 4 ||

**Sameeravani**  

This is one of the auspicious introductory verses, praying to Goddess **Bhāratī (the consort of Vāyu)**, who is the presiding deity of speech and thought.

**1. Primary Meaning (General Connection of the Verse)**  
**Yadanubhāvād** (by whose grace or power) **eḍamūko'pi** (even a person who is deaf and mute) **vāgmī bhavati** (becomes an eloquent speaker), **jaḍamatirapi jantur jāyate prājñamauliḥ** (even a dull-witted creature becomes a crown among scholars); **sā sakalavacanacetodevatā bhāratī mama vacasi mānase ca sannidhiṃ nidhattām** (may Goddess Bharati, the ruler of all speech and thoughts, reside in my words and mind).

**2. Verse Analysis according to Commentary**  
The **commentary (Pa.)** explains the deep meaning and grammar of this verse as follows:

* **I. The Basis of Auspicious Invocation (Teaching Etiquette)**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"devīṃ sarasvatīṃ vyāsamiti śiṣṭācāraśikṣāmanusṛtya vāyupatnīsannidhimarthyate ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** Just as Vedavyasa and other great sages pay respects to the deities before starting a work to establish a model of good conduct for the world, the Acārya prays for the presence of Goddess Bharati, the consort of Vayu.

* **II. Derivation of the Word 'Edamuka'**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"eḍamūkaḥ vaktuṃ śrotuṃ ca aśaktaḥ 'jaḍo'jña eḍamūkastu vaktuṃ śrotu maśikṣita' ityamarokteḥ ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** The commentator quotes the *Amarakosha* to clarify that 'Edamuka' refers to a person who is naturally blind, deaf, or mute, and completely unable to speak or hear.

* **III. The Significance of the Word 'Api' (A Fortiori Argument / Kaimutya Nyaya)**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"kimbvanīdṛśa iti kaimutyārtho'piśabdaḥ ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** The use of **'api'** in 'eḍamūko'pi' (even a deaf-mute) and 'jaḍamatirapi' (even a dull-witted creature) represents **Kaimutya Nyaya** (argument *a fortiori*). If by Goddess Bharati's grace even a mute person can become a great orator, then what need is there to speak of naturally intelligent people? She will surely grant them eloquence.

* **IV. Cause-and-Effect Relationship (Indicating the Reason)**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"vāgmitvabhavane prājñamaulmitvabhavane ca hetuḥ sakalavacanacetodevate-ti ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** How is this miracle possible that a mute becomes eloquent and a dull-witted person becomes a scholar? The reason is given in the fourth line of the verse—because she is the **'sakalavacanacetodevata'** (the presiding deity of all speech and thoughts). Since she controls speech, intellect, and mind, she is capable of accomplishing everything.

**3. Conclusion**  
According to this commentary, praying for Goddess Bharati's presence in speech and mind ensures that the composed work (Nyayasudha) will be rich in expression (eloquent) and full of profound meaning (understood by scholars), leading to the spiritual welfare of seekers of liberation.

ramānivāsocitavāsabhūmiḥ sannyāyaratnāvalijanmabhūmiḥ |  
vairāgyabhāgyo mama padmanābhatīrthāmṛtābdhir bhavatād vibhūtyai || 5 ||

**Sameeravani**  

This verse respectfully remembers **Śrīpad Padmanābhatīrtha Śrīcaraṇa** (a direct disciple of Śrīmad Madhvācārya), the earliest commentator on the *Anuvyākhyāna*. Through **Śleṣa Alaṅkāra** (pun), Śrī Padmanābhatīrtha is compared to the **'ocean of milk' (amṛtābdhi)**.

**1. Primary Meaning (General Connection)**  
**Ramānivāsocitavāsabhūmiḥ** (a fit dwelling place for Goddess Lakshmi; in the Acārya's case - the abode of devotion to God), **sannyāyaratnāvalijanmabhūmiḥ** (the creator of the commentary named 'Sannyayaratnavali'; in the ocean's case - the source of beautiful gems), **vairāgyabhāgyo** (endowed with renunciation; in the ocean's case - having a white, pure luster) **mama padmanābhatīrthāmṛtābdhiḥ** (my ocean of nectar in the form of Padmanabha Tirtha) **vibhūtyai bhavatād** (may he lead to my prosperity and knowledge).

**2. Verse Analysis according to Commentary**  
The commentaries explain the depth of this verse from two perspectives (the Acārya and the Ocean of Nectar):

* **I. Invocation of the Ancient Commentator**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"prācīnaṭīkākārādabhīṣṭamāśāste... anuvyākhyānasya pūrvaṭīkā sannyāyaratnāvalī. tatkartṛn padmanābhatīrthaśrīmac Caraṇān kṣīrasamudrasāmyena varṇayan jñānādyaiśvaryasādhanatvaṃ prārthayate ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** The commentator clarifies that the first commentary on the *Anuvyakhyana* is **'Sannyayaratnavali'**. To show respect to its author, Srimad Padmanabha Tirtha, the author compares him to the ocean of milk and prays for his blessings to attain spiritual wealth, knowledge, devotion, and detachment.

* **II. Meaning of 'Ramānivāsocitavāsabhūmiḥ'**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"krameṇa viśeṣaṇatrayeṇa bhaktijñānavairāgyāṇi vyañjayanti ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** The three adjectives indicate the main virtues of a seeker: Devotion, Knowledge, and Detachment.
    * **For the Acārya (Devotion):** 'Ramā' refers to Lakshmi, who represents supreme devotion to God. Hence, a fit dwelling place for Lakshmi means their heart is the shelter of supreme devotion.
    * **For the Ocean:** The ocean of milk is the dwelling place of Lakshmi and Narayana.

* **III. Meaning of 'Sannyāyaratnāvalijanmabhūmiḥ'**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"sannyāyaratnāvalyāḥ anuvyākhyānaṭīkāgranthasya janmabhūmiḥ janaka ityarthaḥ ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:**
    * **For the Acārya (Knowledge):** Padmanabha Tirtha is the creator of the famous commentary 'Sannyayaratnavali', which is filled with gems of logical arguments. This shows his deep knowledge.
    * **For the Ocean:** The ocean is naturally the birthplace of gems (Ratnakara).

* **IV. Meaning of 'Vairāgyabhāgyo'**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"abdhipakṣe vairāgyaṃ śauklyaṃ dhyeyam ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:**
    * **For the Acārya (Detachment):** He is a renouncer of all worldly attachments, endowed with supreme detachment (Vairagya).
    * **For the Ocean:** In the case of the ocean, 'vairāgya' is interpreted as **'śauklyam'** (whiteness/purity). The ocean of milk is pure white.

* **V. Meaning of 'Vibhūtyai'**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"jñānādyaiśvaryasādhanatvaṃ prārthayate ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** 'Vibhuti' means prosperity or wealth. In a spiritual context, it refers to the divine wealth of knowledge, devotion, and detachment, which the Acārya prays to receive through the grace of Srimad Padmanabha Tirtha.

**3. Conclusion**  
The commentary implies that Srimad Padmanabha Tirtha's *Sannyayaratnavali* is the primary foundation for uncovering the hidden meanings of the *Anuvyakhyana* (**"sannyāyaratnāvalīgranthasyātropajīvanaṃ vyañjayan..."**). Therefore, saluting him as an ocean of nectar serves to complete the work without obstacles.

padavākyapramāṇajñān prativādimadacchidaḥ | śrīmadakṣobhyatīrthākhyān upatiṣṭhe gurūn mama || 6 || 

**Sameeravani**  

This verse pays respectful homage to the author's direct spiritual preceptor (vidyā-guru), **Śrīmad Akṣobhyatīrtha Śrīcaraṇa**.

**1. Primary Meaning (General Connection)**  
I worship in my mind (**upatiṣṭhe**) my preceptor (**gurun mama**), named **Śrīmad Akṣobhyatīrtha**, who is well-versed in grammar, Mimamsa, and logic (**padavākyapramāṇajñān**), and who destroys the pride of rival disputants (**prativādimadacchidaḥ**).

**2. Verse Analysis according to Commentary**  
The commentaries explain the grammatical correctness and philosophical meaning of this verse:

* **I. Salute to the Guru**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"svagurūnnamati ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** The commentator clarifies that the author (Sri Jayatirtha) salutes his direct spiritual master, Srimad Akshobhya Tirtha, who initiated him into Sannyasa and taught him the scriptures, to ensure the obstacle-free completion of the work.

* **II. Meaning of 'Padavākyapramāṇajñān' (Mastery of Three Shastras)**  
  The commentators define 'pada', 'vakya', and 'pramana' as follows:
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"padaṃ vyākaraṇam । vākyaṃ mīmāṃsā । pramāṇaṃ nyāyaḥ ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** 
    * **Pada** refers to **Grammar (Vyakarana)**, which determines the correctness of words.
    * **Vakya** refers to **Mimamsa**, which determines the syntax and interpretation of sentences.
    * **Pramana** refers to **Logic (Nyaya/Tarka)**, which determines the validity of knowledge.
    This shows that Akshobhya Tirtha possessed exceptional scholarship in all three main fields of study.

* **III. The Grammatical Specialty of 'Upatiṣṭhe' (Atmanepada Process)**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"upatiṣṭhe manasā pūjayāmi । 'upānmantrakaraṇa' iti sūtre 'upāddevapūjāsaṅgatikaraṇamitrakaraṇapathiṣvi'ti vārtikena pūjāyāmātmanepadam ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** Here, 'upatiṣṭhe' means "I worship in my mind". In Panini's grammar, under the rule **'upānmantrakaraṇe' (1.3.25)** and the Vartika **'upāddevapūjāsaṅgatikaraṇamitrakaraṇapathiṣu vaktavyam'**, when worship is indicated, the root 'stha' takes **Atmanepada** instead of Parasmaipada. Thus, the form 'upatiṣṭhe' is grammatically correct and appropriate.

**3. Conclusion**  
Srimad Akshobhya Tirtha was not just a lineage head, but a scholar of the highest order in grammar, Mimamsa, and logic. Worshiping such a storehouse of knowledge with a pure mind elevates the dignity and authority of this work.

śrīmadānandatīrthāryasanmanaḥsarasībhuvi | anuvyākhyānanaline cañcarīkati me manaḥ || 7 || 

**Sameeravani**  

This verse expresses Sri Jayatirtha's resolve and purpose in writing the commentary. Through **Rūpaka Alaṅkāra** (metaphor), his mind is compared to a **bee (cañcarīka)**.

**1. Primary Meaning (General Connection)**  
My mind (**me manaḥ**) acts like a bee (**cañcarīkati**) on the lotus of the Anuvyakhyana (**anuvyākhyānanaline**) which has blossomed in the lake of the pure minds of Srimad Anandatirtha and other saintly people (**śrīmadānandatīrthāryasanmanaḥsarasībhūvi**).

**2. Verse Analysis according to Commentary**  
The commentaries explain the deep grammar and metaphor of this verse:

* **I. Declaration of Intent**
  * **Explanation:** Having saluted the Guru lineage, the author now declares his goal to write a commentary (Nyayasudha) on the *Anuvyakhyana* to extract its deep meaning.

* **II. Metaphor of 'Śrīmadānandatīrthāryasanmanaḥsarasībhūvi'**
  * **Commentary Quote (Pa.):** *"sanmanorūpasarasyāṃ sarovare bhuvi jāta ityarthaḥ ॥ 'tṛtīyādiṣu bhāṣitapuṃskaṃ puṃvadgālavasya'ti puṃvadbhāvaḥ ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:**
    * **Metaphor:** The pure mind of Srimad Anandatirtha and other noble saints is a divine lake (Sarasi). The *Anuvyakhyana* is a lotus born in that lake, and the author's mind acts like a bee hovering over it to drink its nectar.
    * **Grammar:** For 'sarasībhūvi', Panini's rule **'tṛtīyādiṣu bhāṣitapuṃskaṃ puṃvadgālavasya' (7.1.74)** permits the masculine treatment of the feminine word 'sarasī' before the suffix, resulting in the correct form.

* **III. Gender of 'Anuvyākhyānanaline'**
  * **Commentary Quote (Ya.):** *"vastutastu 'mānyānāmibhānapuline'tyādipulliṅgānuśāsanasūtreṇa nalinaśabdasya pulliṅgatā'bhidhānādidaṃ viśeṣaṇamupapannamiti dhyeyam ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** The commentator discusses the gender of the word 'nalina' (lotus). According to the rules of masculine gender words, 'nalina' can be masculine, hence the locative masculine form 'anuvyākhyānanaline' is grammatically correct.

* **IV. The Complex Derivation of 'Cañcarīkati'**
  * **Commentary Quote (Sa.):** *"cañcarīkatīti । rolambaścañcarīkaścetyabhidhānāt cañcarīko bhramaraḥ, tadvadācaratītyarthaḥ । atra carateryaṅlugantāt 'caraphalośce'tyanenābhyāsasya nugvidhānena tasya cānusvāropalakṣaṇatvenānusvāre īkakpratyaye cañcarīka iti jāte tasmātsarvaprātipadikebhyaḥ kvibācara ityanena kvipi tallope 'sanādyantādhātava' ityanena dhātusaṃjñāyāṃ tiṅutpattau cañcarīkatīti rūpasiddhiḥ..."*  
  * **Explanation:** 
    1. **Meaning:** According to lexicographers, 'cañcarīka' means a **bee**.
    2. **Derivation:** From the verbal root 'car' (to move), in the intensive (yaṅ-luk) form, with the duplication prefix receiving the 'nuk' augment, and the suffix 'īkak', the noun 'cañcarīka' is derived.
    3. **Denominative Verb (Acara-Kvip):** By the rule *"sarvaprātipadikebhya ācāre kvip vaktavyaḥ"* (Kvip is added to nouns to mean "acting like..."), a denominative verb is created. After dropping the Kvip suffix, it gets the status of a verb root, and under Present Tense (Lat) third-person singular (Tip), the form **'cañcarīkati'** is derived. It means "acts like a bee in search of nectar".

**3. Conclusion**  
Just as a bee hovers around a lotus to drink its nectar, Srimad Jayatirtha's mind is eager to taste the profound spiritual nectar of the *Anuvyakhyana* arising from Srimad Madhvacharya's heart. This expresses the author's intense devotion and focus.

na śabdābdhau gāḍhā na ca nigamacarcāsu caturā na ca nyāye prauḍhā na ca viditavedyā api vayam | paraṃ śrīmatpūrṇapramatigurukāruṇyasaraṇiṃ prapannā mānyāḥ smaḥ kimapi vadanto'pi mahatām || 8 ||

**Sameeravani**  

This verse was composed by Sri Jayatirtha to express his profound humility and to highlight the real source of strength behind his commentary.

**1. Primary Meaning (General Connection)**  
We are **not deep in the ocean of words** (we are not masters of grammar), **nor are we clever in discussions of the Vedas** (we are not experts in Mimamsa), **nor are we mature in logic** (we are not skilled in Nyaya Shastra), **nor have we known all that is to be known** (we are not omniscient); **yet** (but), having taken shelter in the path of compassion of our guru, the all-knowing Srimad Anandatirtha (**śrīmatpūrṇapramatigurukāruṇyasaraṇiṃ prapannāḥ**), we become respected (**mānyāḥ smaḥ**) even when speaking something (**kimapi vadanto'pi**) before great assemblies of scholars (**mahatām**).

**2. Verse Analysis according to Commentary**

* **I. Expression of Humility (Vinaya)**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"cañcarīkatītyuktayā prāptaṃ svamahattvaṃ vinayena bhūṣayituṃ vinayapradarśanaṃ karoti ॥ na śabdeti ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** In the seventh verse, by saying "my mind is like a bee on the Anuvyakhyana", the author hinted at his profound scholarship. To adorn that greatness with humility and avoid any pride, he expresses extreme humility here through "na śabdābdhau...".

* **II. Meaning of 'Nigamacarcāsu'**
  * **Explanation:** 'Nigama' refers to the Vedas. The author states that they are not experts in the Mimamsa analysis of Vedic sentences.

* **III. The Main Strength of the Commentary (Guru's Grace)**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"kacidapi cenna prauḍhistarhi kiṃ balaṃ vyākhyāne ityata āha ॥ paramiti ॥ anyadbalamastītyarthaḥ । tarhi gurukāruṇyasaraṇiṃ prapannā iti yattadeva balaṃ ato mānyā ityannvayaḥ ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** If the author claims no mastery in grammar, Mimamsa, or logic, what is the strength behind composing such a monumental commentary as the *Nyayasudha*? The answer is given by **'param'** (but) — the unconditional flow of compassion of Srimad Madhvacharya (**pūrṇapramatiguru**). That grace alone is the author's strength, making their words acceptable to scholars.

* **IV. Indication of Original Intent (Mula-Prameya)**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"etena agre vakṣyamāṇaṃ sarvaṃ mūlābhipretamiti dhvanitam ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** By relying entirely on the Guru's grace, it is implied that whatever doctrines are explained in the *Nyayasudha* are in complete agreement with the original intent of the author (Madhvacharya), and are not the commentator's own fabrications.

* **V. Interpretation of 'Kimapi' and 'Vayam'**
  * **Explanation:** 
    * **Kimapi:** Whether correct or incorrect, whatever we speak. This is an expression of deep humility.
    * **Plural form 'Vayam' (We):** Even though there is a single speaker, the plural 'we' is used. This represents the abundance of qualities (like grammar and logic) required for writing a commentary, which have been attained through the Guru's grace. It could also refer to the dual systems of thought explained.

* **VI. Brevity to Avoid Excess Volume**
  * **Commentary Quote:** *"granthagauravabhayātsaṅkṣepaḥ । prativākyaṃ pratipadamanekākṛtagarbhītā । pratibhāti sudhā'thāpi granthālpatvāya nocyate ॥"*  
  * **Explanation:** At the end of the invocation, the commentator remarks that every word and sentence of the *Nyayasudha* contains deep, hidden imports. Though the *Nyayasudha* is filled with brilliance, the details are summarized here to prevent the book from becoming excessively bulky.

**3. Conclusion**  
Sri Jayatirtha, despite his unmatched mastery of all scriptures, dedicates all his learning to the grace of Srimad Madhvacharya. The Guru's grace is the ultimate secret behind the authority and depth of the *Nyayasudha*.

**Nyayasudha**   
Bhagavata badarayanena praninam nihsreyasaya praneetamapi brahmameemansasastramasadhunibandhamandhatamasa avakundhitatvenapraneetamiva manyamano bhagavan anandatheertha- muniryatha''charyabhiprayam asya bhasyam vidhaya anubhasyamapi karisyanvileenaprakrititaya svayamantharayavidhuro'pyanavaratameesvarapravanakarayaranavrittirapi narayanapranamadikam praripsitasayanantharaya- parisamapteh prachayasya cha hetutaya'vigeetasistacharaparamparadina'vagatamavasyam karaneeyam sisyan grahayitum granthadau nibadhnati ॥ Narayanamiti ।

**Sameeravani**   
This introductory passage explains the context of Srimad Anandatirtha's commentary (Bhashya and Anubhashya) on the Brahmasutras. The detailed explanation in simple English is summarized in the following points:

**1. The Purpose of Commencing the Bhashya**
* **The Original Text's Purpose:** Lord Badarayana (Sri Vedavyasa) composed the 'Brahma Mimamsa Shastra' (Brahmasutras) for the ultimate liberation (moksha) of all living beings. However, prior commentaries by incorrect authors (faulty treatises) covered the real meaning of the text in the thick darkness of ignorance.
* **The Resolution:** Because of this, the power of the scriptures to generate valid knowledge was obstructed, making the scriptures appear as if they were never composed. To remove this doubt about the futility of the texts, Lord Anandatirtha Muni (Srimad Madhvacharya) set out to write the Bhashya and Anubhashya, strictly following the true intent of the author (Vedavyasa).

**2. The Justification for the Auspicious Invocation (Mangalacharana)**
A question arises: Why should a person who is already omniscient and free from all obstacles perform an invocation? The answer is twofold:
* **(A) Obstacle-free Completion of the Work:** Srimad Madhvacharya, being of a 'liberated nature' (**vileenaprakrititaya**), is entirely free from obstacles (**antharayavidhura**). His physical, mental, and vocal activities are constantly dedicated to the Lord. Yet, to ensure the smooth completion of the work and its wide dissemination (**prachaya**), he includes the salutation to Lord Narayana at the beginning.
* **(B) Teaching the Disciples:** The master sets an example. If the guru does not perform the invocation, the disciples will not learn to do so. To teach this essential duty of righteous conduct (Sishta-achara) to the disciples, the salutation is recorded at the start of the book.

**3. The Tradition of Righteous Conduct (Avigeeta-Sishta-Achara)**
The term **'Avigeeta'** indicates that the tradition is Vedic and undisputed:
* **Precedent:** Great sages like Vyasa and Manu have always performed invocations at the beginning of their works. This is a universally accepted, unblemished custom (**avigeeta-sishtachara-parampara**).
* **Inference of Vedic Origin:** Although there is no explicit Vedic commandment saying "one must perform an invocation", the constant practice of selfless, noble saints implies the existence of a corresponding Vedic injunction. This method of inferring Vedic sources is explained in detail later in the commentary.

**4. Summary of Terms**

| Original Word / Sentence | Explanation in English |
| :--- | :--- |
| **bhasyadivaiyarthyasankam niraha** | Dispels the objection regarding the necessity of a new commentary. |
| **asameecinagranthavyandhatamasena** | Covered by the thick darkness of incorrect commentaries. |
| **yathacharyabhiprayam** | According to the true intent of the composer, Sri Vedavyasa. |
| **pranamadikam... sisyan grahayitum** | Performing salutations to teach correct conduct and discipline to disciples. |
| **avigeetaiti** | Unblemished, accepted by the lineage of virtuous people. |

**Conclusion:** Having established the necessity and authority of the invocation, Srimad Madhvacharya begins the auspicious verse with the words — **"Narayanamiti"**, representing the salutation to Lord Narayana.

**Anuvyakhyanam**   
nārāyaṇaṃ nikhila pūrṇaguṇaikadehaṃ nirdoṣam āpyatamam apy akhilaiḥ suvākyaiḥ |
asyodbhavād idam aśeṣviśeṣato'pi vandyaṃ sadā priyatamaṃ mama sannamāmi || 9 ||

**Sameeravani -**   
This is the opening auspicious verse of the **'Anuvyakhyana'** composed by Srimad Anandatirtha Bhagavatpada.

**Word Connection (Anvaya):**  
I salute (**sannamāmi**) Lord Narayana, who is the embodiment of infinite, complete, and auspicious attributes (**nikhilapūrṇaguṇaicadeham**), who is completely free of defects (**nirdoṣam**), who is the primary subject to be known through all noble words of the scriptures (**akhilaiḥ suvākyaiḥ āpyatamam api**), who is the creator of the creation, sustenance, and dissolution of this universe (**asyodbhavādidam**), who is worshiped by all above all else (**aśeṣaviśeṣato'pi vandyam**), and who is always my most beloved (**sadā priyatamaṃ mama**).

**Word-by-Word Meanings:**
* **नारायणम्** (nārāyaṇam) – To Lord Narayana (Second case, singular).
* **निखिलपूर्णगुणैकदेहम्** (nikhilapūrṇaguṇaicadeham) – He whose body (form) is solely composed of complete, infinite, auspicious attributes.
* **निर्दोषम्** (nirdoṣam) – He who is completely free from all flaws.
* **आप्यतमम्** (āpyatamam) – The most worthy of being attained (among all goals of liberation).
* **अपि** (api) – Also/even.
* **अखिलैः** (akhilaiḥ) – By all (Third case, plural).
* **सुवाक्यैः** (suvākyaiḥ) – By beautiful sentences, i.e., the Vedas, Upanishads, and sacred scriptures.
* **अस्य** (asya) – Of this universe (Sixth case, singular).
* **उद्भवादिदम्** (udbhavādidam) – He who grants creation, sustenance, dissolution, etc.
* **अशेषविशेषतः** (aśeṣaviśeṣataḥ) – Completely and specifically.
* **वन्द्यम्** (vandyam) – Worthy of salutations and worship.
* **सदा** (sadā) – Always.
* **प्रियतमम्** (priyatamam) – The most beloved (the ultimate object of love).
* **मम** (mama) – My (Sixth case of 'Asmad').
* **सन्नमामि** (sannamāmi) – I salute with deep devotion (Present tense, first-person singular).

**Detailed Spiritual Commentary:**  
In this invocation, Acharya Madhva salutes his chosen deity, Lord Narayana, and describes His auspicious attributes:
1. **Nikhila-purna-guna-eka-deham:** Worldly beings possess bodies made of five physical elements (flesh, blood, etc.) which are temporary. Lord Narayana's form is not like that; His body is composed purely of infinite, auspicious attributes like knowledge, bliss, and power. He is both the possessor of attributes and the attributes themselves.
2. **Nirdosham:** All worldly beings are afflicted by some flaw (ignorance, sorrow, birth, death, fear). Narayana is completely free from all defects and is eternally pure.
3. **Akhilaih suvakyaih apyatamam:** All the Vedas, Upanishads, Puranas, and histories (like the Mahabharata) directly or indirectly describe and lead to the knowledge of Narayana. He is the ultimate subject of all scriptures.
4. **Asyodbhavadidam-ashesha-visheshatah:** 'Asya' refers to the universe. Narayana alone performs the eightfold functions of the universe: creation, sustenance, dissolution, control, knowledge, ignorance, bondage, and liberation. He is the ruler of all cause-and-effect relationships.
5. **Vandyam sada priyatamam mama:** Lord Narayana is worshiped by all. He is always the most beloved (**priyatama**) to 'me' (the Acharya and the devotee), because the Supreme Lord is the true friend and soul of every being.

**Conclusion:** I (Madhvacharya) salute with body, mind, and speech Lord Narayana, who is filled with infinite attributes, free from all defects, described by all the Vedas, the creator of the universe's creation, sustenance, and dissolution, and my most beloved worshipful Lord. May this salutation bring about the obstacle-free completion of the *Anuvyakhyana*.

**Nyayasudha**   
Atra narayanam sannamameetyanvayah । uttamapurusaprayogadevahameeti labhyate । samiti bhaktyadayatisayasahityalaksanam pranamasya samyaktamah । tathavidha eva hi narayanapranamo bhavatyabhimatasiddherangam । nikhiletyadeenam tu pranamakarmana narayanena visesanataya sambandhah । prayojanam cha stuteepadanam visesyaprasansaiva । tatrarthikapunarukteh stuteetvameva samadhanamevadhaneevyam ।  

**Sameeravani**  

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 1:** *Atra narayanam sannamameetyanvayah .*  
  **Guru:** O child, to understand the primary meaning of the opening verse of the *Anuvyakhyana* composed by Srimad Anandatirtha, we must first look at the main sentence structure. Sri Jayatirtha points out: **"Atra narayanam sannamameetyanvayah"** (The core connection here is 'I salute Narayana'). Although there are many adjectives, they all culminate in the act of saluting Lord Narayana.
  **Shishya:** O master, in our Dvaita philosophy, the supreme, independent entity is Lord Narayana. Hence, it is appropriate that He is the primary object of our salutations.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 2:** *Uttamapurusaprayogadevahameeti labhyate .*  
  **Guru:** Look, my disciple, the word 'Aham' (I) is not explicitly written in the verse. Yet, **"uttamapurusaprayogadevahameeti labhyate"** (the pronoun 'I' is obtained from the use of the first-person singular verb 'namami'). According to grammar rules, a first-person verb always connects with 'I'. Thus, we know the saluter is Srimad Madhvacharya himself.
  **Shishya:** Indeed, Guru, it seems the Acharya used the first-person singular to express his personal dedication and servitude to the Lord.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 3:** *Samiti bhaktyadayatisayasahityalaksanam pranamasya samyaktamah .*  
  **Guru:** In our philosophy, a mere physical bow is not the main thing. **"samiti bhaktyadayatisayasahityalaksanam pranamasya samyaktamah"** means that the prefix 'sam' (in sannamami) represents the quality of being 'complete' or 'proper', which is characterized by intense devotion and knowledge of the Lord's greatness. Sincerity and wisdom are the marks of this salutation.
  **Shishya:** A beautiful explanation! The intense devotion towards Lord Narayana is our main spiritual path, and the prefix 'sam' indicates that.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 4:** *Tathavidha eva hi narayanapranamo bhavatyabhimatasiddherangam .*  
  **Guru:** Child, obstacles are not destroyed by a casual greeting. **"tathavidha eva hi narayanapranamo bhavatyabhimatasiddherangam"**. Only a salutation performed with knowledge and devotion to the independent Lord Narayana acts as the means (**anga**) for completing the work successfully. This is the main purpose of the invocation.
  **Shishya:** Dvaita philosophy's secret is that all human achievements are attained only through the grace of Lord Narayana.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 5:** *Nikhiletyadeenam tu pranamakarmana narayanena visesanataya sambandhah .*  
  **Guru:** Where do the other words in the verse go? **"nikhiletyadeenam tu pranamakarmana narayanena visesanataya sambandhah"**. Words like 'nikhilapurnagunaicadeham' and 'nirdosham' are adjectives qualifying the object 'Narayanam', describing His unique nature.
  **Shishya:** So these adjectives define the characteristics of Narayana: His completeness of attributes and absolute freedom from defects.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 6:** *Prayojanam cha stuteepadanam visesyaprasansaiva .*  
  **Guru:** Why are these adjectives used for Narayana? It is said: **"prayojanam cha stuteepadanam visesyaprasansaiva"** (The purpose of these praise-words is to glorify the object described). Glorifying Lord Narayana's attributes strengthens the devotion of His devotees, and devotion leads to liberation.
  **Shishya:** Indeed, singing the glory of His attributes is the highest form of praise, revealing His absolute supremacy.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 7:** *Tatrarthikapunarukteh stuteetvameva samadhanamevadhaneevyam .*  
  **Guru:** A doubt may arise here. Since 'complete in all attributes' is stated, His 'freedom from defects' is automatically implied. Why state 'nirdosham' separately? The answer is: **"tatrarthikapunarukteh stuteetvameva samadhanamevadhaneevyam"**. In a hymn of praise, repeating a meaning for emphasis is not a defect, but an ornament of style. The disciple must pay special attention to this.
  **Shishya:** A very profound point. Describing the Lord's attributes and His lack of defects is an ornament to our ears, and repetition in praise is highly commendable. I have understood this completely.

**Nyayasudha**   
Athava । kuto narayanasya namyatvam । tathatve'pi kuto'nyasu devatasu sateesu tasyaiva namanamityatroktamasesavisesato'pi vandyamiti । visisyate vastvebhiriti visesah asadharanadharmah asesasca te visesasca । apirabhivyaptau । yavanto vandyatvanimittabhuta dharmah tebhyo viseseobhyo'pi hetubhyo vandyam । tatha'sesadvandyatvena samasankitatpadmapadmabhavadejagatato viseso'tisayo'sesavisesassarvottamatvam । tato hetoh sateesvanyasu devatasu vandyamiti । etadubhayam katham narayanasyeatyato'bhihitam nikhiletyadi । trividha hi devata vandya bhavati । visistadhikrutesta cheti । na hi devatavandanam vyasanitaya kriyate । kintu vighnavighatadiprayojanapeksaya । visistaiva devata tasyeste । adhikruta cha svavisayagranthaprabodhadikam sampadayati । vandanam khalu bhaktyadyupetameva saphalam । tacchestayameva devatayamupapadayata iti । 

**Sameeravani**   

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 1 & 2:** *Athava .* and *Kuto narayanasya namyatvam .*  
  **Guru:** Child, the author salutes Lord Narayana. An objection arises: Why is only Narayana saluted? Why are other deities not saluted? To address this, the commentator introduces an alternative explanation with the word **"Athava"** (Or/Alternatively). The question is: **"Kuto narayanasya namyatvam"** (Why is Narayana worthy of salutations?).

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 3:** *Tathatve'pi kuto'nyasu devatasu sateesu tasyaiva namanamityatroktamasesavisesato'pi vandyamiti .*  
  **Guru:** Even if Narayana is worthy of worship (**tathatve'pi**), there are many other deities in the world. **"kuto'nyasu devatasu sateesu tasyaiva namanam"** (Why salute Him alone when other deities exist?). The answer is: **"ityatroktamasesavisesato'pi vandyamiti"**. To answer this, the adjective 'ashesha-visheshato'pi vandyam' (worshiped above all else) is used in the original verse.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 4:** *Visisyate vastvebhiriti visesah asadharanadharmah asesasca te visesasca .*  
  **Shishya:** Respectable master, what is the meaning of the term 'ashesha-visheshatah'?  
  **Guru:** Listen, child. **"visisyate vastvebhiriti visesah asadharanadharmah"** (Those attributes by which an object is distinguished from others are called its unique characteristics or 'visheshah'). **"asesasca te visesasca"** (And those characteristics which are complete and without exception are 'ashesha-visheshah'). All the supreme, unique attributes of the Lord are included here.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 5:** *Apirabhivyaptau .*  
  **Shishya:** Why is the word 'api' used in 'ashesha-visheshato'pi'?  
  **Guru:** Here, **"apirabhivyaptau"** (the word 'api' denotes inclusion or pervasion). It means that no auspicious attribute is excluded.
  **Shishya:** Respected teacher, this grammatical point is fascinating. I did not know that a particle like 'api' could take a visarga. Please explain this in simple terms.
  **Guru:** Listen carefully, child:
  * **General Rule:** **'Api'** is an indeclinable (**Avyaya**), and normally does not take a visarga.
  * **Special Rule (Svarupa-paratva):** When a word itself is discussed as the subject of a sentence, it behaves like a noun (**Nama-pada**).
  * **Declension:** Because it behaves like a noun, the nominative singular case suffix is added to **'api'**, declining it like **'kavi'** to become **'apiḥ'**.
  * **Sandhi:** When combined with 'abhivyaptau' (apiḥ + abhivyaptau), the visarga becomes 'r', resulting in **'apirabhivyaptau'**.
  If the visarga were not used and it was written as 'api abhivyaptau', it would mean "in inclusion also", changing the meaning. Therefore, **'apiḥ'** is declined as a noun to refer specifically to the word 'api' itself.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 6:** *Yavanto vandyatvanimittabhuta dharmah tebhyo viseseobhyo'pi hetubhyo vandyam .*  
  **Guru:** The commentator explains the meaning: **"yavanto vandyatvanimittabhuta dharmah"** (Whatever attributes in the world cause one to be worthy of worship, such as auspicious qualities), **"tebhyo viseseobhyo'pi hetubhyo vandyam"** (Narayana is worshipful due to all those unique attributes). He is saluted because He is filled with all worshipful qualities.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 7:** *Tatha'sesadvandyatvena samasankitatpadmapadmabhavadejagatato viseso'tisayo'sesavisesassarvottamatvam .*  
  **Guru:** There is another meaning to this term. **"tatha'sesadvandyatvena samasankitatpadmapadmabhavadejagatato"** (From all those beings in the universe suspected of being worshipful, such as Lakshmi (Padma) and Brahma (Padmabhava)), **"viseso'tisayo'sesavisesassarvottamatvam"** (Narayana possesses a distinction/superiority, which is 'ashesha-vishesha', meaning Supremacy). The final meaning is that Narayana is worshipful because He is the Supreme Being (Sarvottama).

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 8:** *Tato hetoh sateesvanyasu devatasu vandyamiti .*  
  **Guru:** Because Narayana is the Supreme Being, **"tato hetoh sateesvanyasu devatasu vandyamiti"**. For this reason, even though other deities exist, Narayana alone is primarily worshiped.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 9:** *Etadubhayam katham narayanasyeatyato'bhihitam nikhiletyadi .*  
  **Shishya:** Preceptor, how do both these qualities—infinitude of attributes and absolute supremacy—belong to Narayana alone? Why not to anyone else?  
  **Guru:** A good question. **"etadubhayam katham narayanasyeatyato'bhihitam nikhiletyadi"**. To answer this, the adjective 'nikhila...' is used in the original verse to show that these belong uniquely to Narayana.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 10 & 11:** *Trividha hi devata vandya bhavati .* and *Visistadhikrutesta cheti .*  
  **Guru:** In tradition, the deity to be worshiped is of three kinds: **"trividha hi devata vandya bhavati"**. They are **"visistadhikrutesta cheti"** (the distinguished deity, the presiding/authorized deity, and the beloved/chosen deity).

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 12 & 13:** *Na hi devatavandanam vyasanitaya kriyate .* and *Kintu vighnavighatadiprayojanapeksaya .*  
  **Guru:** Why do people worship a deity? **"na hi devatavandanam vyasanitaya kriyate"** (Worship is not done out of a mere habit or without purpose). **"kintu vighnavighatadiprayojanapeksaya"** (Rather, it is done with the desire to remove obstacles and achieve the desired goals of the work).

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 14 & 15:** *Visistaiva devata tasyeste .* and *Adhikruta cha svavisayagranthaprabodhadikam sampadayati .*  
  **Guru:** Only a deity endowed with all auspicious attributes (**visishta**) is capable of granting desires: **"visistaiva devata tasyeste"**. And the deity authorized for a specific task (**adhikruta**) grants the proper understanding of that subject: **"adhikruta cha svavisayagranthaprabodhadikam sampadayati"** (like the gurus who grant knowledge of the text).

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 16 & 17:** *Vandanam khalu bhaktyadyupetameva saphalam .* and *Tacchestayameva devatayamupapadayata iti .*  
  **Guru:** Furthermore, **"vandanam khalu bhaktyadyupetameva saphalam"** (Salutation is fruitful only when accompanied by true devotion). And where does such devotion arise? **"tacchestayameva devatayamupapadayata iti"** (Devotion is possible only towards one's beloved/chosen deity). Thus, Lord Narayana, possessing all three qualities, is the one saluted in the verse.

**Nyayasudha**   
Tatra vaisistyopadanaya nikhilapurnagunaicadeham nirdosamasyodbhavadidameeti visesanatrayam . nikhila nissesah purnah pratyekamapyanavadhika gunah anandadayah . ekasabdah kevalarthah . ta eva deho yasya na punah prakrutadeeriti tathoktah . niskranto dosebhyah paratantryadeebhyah iti nirdosah . asya pratyaksadeepramanasiddhataya buddhasannihitasya svavyatirekaprapanchasyodbhava utpattiradiryasyetyudbhavadi janmadyastakam tadyathasambhavam dadateetyudbhavadidastam . asyeeti sambandhamatre sasthi . asesavisesato'peetyetadasyodbhavadidameetyanenapi sambadhyate . viyadadhikaranadeevyutpadyasamastavantarabhedasahitodbhavadidameeti .  

**Sameeravani**  

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 1:** *Tatra vaisistyopadanaya nikhilapurnagunaicadeham nirdosamasyodbhavadidameeti visesanatrayam .*  
  **Shishya:** Master, you explained that Narayana is 'distinguished' (visishta). How is this proved?  
  **Guru:** O child, **"tatra vaisistyopadanaya"** (to prove His distinguished nature), the author used three adjectives: **"nikhilapurnagunaicadeham nirdosamasyodbhavadidameeti visesanatrayam"** (1. nikhilapurnagunaicadeham, 2. nirdosham, 3. asyodbhavadidam).

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 2:** *Nikhila nissesah purnah pratyekamapyanavadhika gunah anandadayah .*  
  **Shishya:** What do these terms mean? What are 'nikhilapurnagunah'?  
  **Guru:** **"nikhila nissesah"** means without exception. **"purnah pratyekamapyanavadhika gunah anandadayah"** means that each of His attributes, such as bliss, knowledge, and power, is limitless and complete.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 3 & 4:** *Ekasabdah kevalarthah .* and *Ta eva deho yasya na punah prakrutadeeriti tathoktah .*  
  **Shishya:** What does 'guna-eka-deha' mean?  
  **Guru:** **"ekasabdah kevalarthah"** (The word 'eka' here means 'solely' or 'only'). **"ta eva deho yasya"** (His body is solely made of these attributes). **"na punah prakrutadeeriti tathoktah"** (His body is not physical or material, but purely spiritual).

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 5:** *Niskranto dosebhyah paratantryadeebhyah iti nirdosah .*  
  **Shishya:** What does 'nirdosham' mean? Does He have any faults like worldly beings?  
  **Guru:** No. **"niskranto dosebhyah paratantryadeebhyah iti nirdosah"** (He who is completely free from defects like ignorance, sorrow, and dependence is 'nirdosha').

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 6:** *Asya pratyaksadeepramanasiddhataya buddhasannihitasya svavyatirekaprapanchasyodbhava utpattiradiryasyetyudbhavadi janmadyastakam tadyathasambhavam dadateetyudbhavadidastam .*  
  **Shishya:** Master, 'asyodbhavadidam' is a very long word. Please explain it.  
  **Guru:** This contains a major philosophical doctrine. **"asya pratyaksadeepramanasiddhataya buddhasannihitasya"** ('Asya' refers to that which is established by perception and logic, and is thus present in our intellect). **"svavyatirekaprapanchasya"** (of the universe which is different from Narayana). **"udbhava utpattiradiryasyetyudbhavadi"** (Creation is the first of these functions). This refers to the eightfold functions (**janmadyastakam**): creation, sustenance, dissolution, control, knowledge, ignorance, bondage, and liberation. **"tadyathasambhavam dadateetyudbhavadidastam"** (He who grants these functions to all according to their eligibility is 'udbhavadidah').
  **Shishya:** Why did the author add the adjective **'buddhi-sannihitasya'** (present in the intellect)?
  **Guru:** There are three main reasons:
    1. **Nature of Pronouns:** The pronoun 'asya' (of this) normally refers to something near and visible. Since the vast universe cannot be fully visible at once, the commentator explains that it is **present in our intellect** because it is proven by perception and logic.
    2. **Refuting Illusionism (Mayavada):** Those who claim the world is an illusion cannot claim it is present in the intellect as a reality. By stating **'pramana-siddhataya'** (established by valid proof), the author asserts that the world is real, not an illusion, and its creation is performed by Narayana.
    3. **Object of Creation:** For creation to be explained, the object must be present in the mind. Thus, Narayana performs the eight functions on the real universe present in our intellect.

  **Shishya:** How does He perform all eight functions simultaneously? Are they not contradictory?  
  **Guru:** That is why the word **'yathasambhavam'** (as applicable) is used.
    * **Inanimate Matter (Jada):** Only creation, sustenance, dissolution, and control apply.
    * **Liberated Souls (Muktas):** Only sustenance, control, and knowledge/bliss apply.
    * **Bound Souls (Baddhas):** Bondage, ignorance, and liberation apply according to their eligibility.
    Thus, the Lord maintains the order of the universe without violating the nature of things.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 7:** *Asyeeti sambandhamatre sasthi .*  
  **Guru:** In 'asya udbhavadi', **"asyeeti sambandhamatre sasthi"** (The genitive case 'asya' represents a general relationship between the creator and the created).

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 8 & 9:** *Asesavisesato'peetyetadasyodbhavadidameetyanenapi sambadhyate .* and *Viyadadhikaranadeevyutpadyasamastavantarabhedasahitodbhavadidameeti .*  
  **Guru:** The term 'ashesha-visheshatah' also connects with 'asyodbhavadidam': **"asesavisesato'peetyetadasyodbhavadidameetyanenapi sambadhyate"**. This yields: **"viyadadhikaranadeevyutpadyasamastavantarabhedasahitodbhavadidameeti"**. Narayana creates not just the gross universe, but all internal divisions like space, air, and primordial nature as detailed in the Brahmasutras.

**Nyayasudha**   
Adhikrutatvapradarsanayapyatamamapyakhilaih suvakyairiti . apisabdo vandyatve hetusamucchayarthah . abhivyaptau va . apauruseyatvena tanmulatvena va'nasankitadosatvadina sobhanani vakyani vedadeeni brahmasutradeeni cha suvakyani tairakhilairapyatamam . atisayena pratipadyam . yadi vyachikhyasitena brahmameemansasastrena tannirnaitavyarthena vedadina cha pratipadya iti narayano vandaneeyah . hanta tarhi tata eva dharmadikam pranadikam va vandaneeyam syat . na syat . tasya narayanapratipattyangataya vedayaikadesapratipadyatvat . asya punarananyarthataya'khilavedadivedyatvat . tathavidhameva chadhikrutamuchyata ityasayavata''pyatamamityakhilairiti choktam . istatvapradarsanam sada priyatamam mameti . atrapi purvavatsadapadasya tamapascha prayojanamavadheyam .  

**Sameeravani**  

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 1:** *Adhikrutatvapradarsanayapyatamamapyakhilaih suvakyairiti .*  
  **Guru:** Child, to show that Narayana is the **presiding subject** (adhikruta) of all scriptures, the author used the adjectives: **"apyatamamapyakhilaih suvakyairiti"** (the most worthy of being attained through all noble words). He is the primary subject matter of all Vedas, Upanishads, and Brahmasutras.
  **Shishya:** But the Vedas mention fire (Agni), Indra, wind (Vayu), and sacrifices. How is Narayana the sole primary subject?
  **Guru:** This is understood through **'Parama-mukhya-vritti'** (the supreme primary significance):
    * **Inner Controller:** Names like Agni and Indra ultimately refer to Narayana as the inner controller of those deities.
    * **Subordination of Other Topics:** Sacrifices and other rituals are only secondary means (**anga**) to purify the mind, which leads to the knowledge of Narayana.
    * **Independent Purpose (Ananyarthatva):** Other topics serve the knowledge of Narayana, but Narayana is the independent, final goal.
  Just as all rivers flow to the ocean, all scriptures ultimately reveal Narayana.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 2 & 3:** *Apisabdo vandyatve hetusamucchayarthah .* and *Abhivyaptau va .*  
  **Guru:** The word 'api' in 'apyatamam-api' is either for adding another reason for His worshipfulness (**hetu-samucchaya**) or for indicating pervasion (**abhivyapti**).

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 4 & 5:** *Apauruseyatvena tanmulatvena va'nasankitadosatvadina sobhanani vakyani vedadeeni brahmasutradeeni cha suvakyani tairakhilairapyatamam .* and *Atisayena pratipadyam .*  
  **Guru:** What are 'suvakyani'? **"apauruseyatvena tanmulatvena va'nasankitadosatvadina sobhanani vakyani vedadeeni brahmasutradeeni cha suvakyani"** (Vedas are noble because they are eternal, and Brahmasutras are noble because they are based on the Vedas. They are free from defects like delusion). **"tairakhilairapyatamam"** means He is the ultimate subject of all of them. **"atisayena pratipadyam"** means He is described in the highest degree, as the crown jewel of all scriptures.

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 6 & 7:** *Yadi vyachikhyasitena brahmameemansasastrena tannirnaitavyarthena vedadina cha pratipadya iti narayano vandaneeyah .* and *Hanta tarhi tata eva dharmadikam pranadikam va vandaneeyam syat .*  
  **Guru:** An objector argues: **"yadi vyachikhyasitena brahmameemansasastrena tannirnaitavyarthena vedadina cha pratipadya iti narayano vandaneeyah"** (If Narayana is worshipful because He is described by the scriptures), then: **"hanta tarhi tata eva dharmadikam pranadikam va vandaneeyam syat"** (Why not worship dharma or prana, which are also described in the scriptures?).

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 8 & 9:** *Na syat .* and *Tasya narayanapratipattyangataya vedayaikadesapratipadyatvat .*  
  **Guru:** The author replies: **"na syat"** (This is not so). Why? **"tasya narayanapratipattyangataya vedayaikadesapratipadyatvat"** (Because dharma and other topics are described only in portions of the Vedas, and they serve only as secondary means to gain the knowledge of Narayana).

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 10 & 11:** *Asya punarananyarthataya'khilavedadivedyatvat .* and *Tathavidhameva chadhikrutamuchyata ityasayavata''pyatamamityakhilairiti choktam .*  
  **Guru:** On the other hand: **"asya punarananyarthataya'khilavedadivedyatvat"** (Narayana is the independent subject of all scriptures). **"tathavidhameva chadhikrutamuchyata"** (Such an independent subject is called the 'presiding subject'). **"ityasayavata''pyatamamityakhilairiti choktam"** (With this intent, the words 'apyatamam' and 'akhilaih' were used).

* **Nyayasudha Sentence 12 & 13:** *Istatvapradarsanam sada priyatamam mameti .* and *Atrapi purvavatsadapadasya tamapascha prayojanamavadheyam .*  
  **Guru:** Narayana's nature as the **beloved deity** is shown by: **"istatvapradarsanam sada priyatamam mameti"** (He is always my most beloved). Here also: **"atrapi purvavatsadapadasya tamapascha prayojanamavadheyam"** (The word 'sada' denotes eternity of love, and the suffix 'tamap' shows that His beloved nature is supreme above all others). This establishes Narayana's nature as the chosen deity.
