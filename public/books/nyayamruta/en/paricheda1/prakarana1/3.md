 Separator

In jurisprudence, especially in **new law**, there is a very important terminology called 'deductive' A detailed description of this is given below for example:
1. Definition of Avacchedaka:
The metallic meaning of 'separator' is \- **'distinguisher'** or **'limitor or Distinguisher'**. A religion that distinguishes a substance from others and determines the nature of that substance is called a 'separator'

* **Avacchedaka:** The Delimitor/Property.
* **Avacchedya:** The entity being delimited.

2. The need for an isolator (Why is it needed?):
The separator is used to correct the errors of **'Over-extension'** and **'Under-extension'** when discussing a subject in Scripture. This brings 'precision' to knowledge.

3. Key Examples:
a. Simple Object Example:
When we say \- **"This is a pot," how does our intellect have knowledge of the pot?

* There is a property called 'Pot-ness' in the pot.
* This very 'potentiality' is the **'separator'** here.
* This religion separates the pot from the cloth, pillars, etc.
* Here the 'pot' is **'detachable'**, and the 'pot' is **'detachable'**.

b. In the context of Negation/Abhāva:
In jurisprudence, the 'deductive' plays an important role in the discussion of deficiency.
The sentence \- **"There is no pot on the ground"** (There is no pot on the ground).

* Who is missing here? of the pot. Therefore, the pot is a 'competitor'
* Which religion of the pot is forbidden here? 'potentiality'
* Therefore, 'being a counter-positive' is here called **'The delimitor of being a counter-positive.
* If I say "Blue pot is not here", then 'blueness-specific potness' will be the deductive.

c. In Logic/Inference:
The famous conjecture \- **"The mountain has fire because of smoke"** (The mountain has fire because of smoke).
1. **Delimitor of subject-hood:** 'Mountainousness' (We’re talking about all the mountains, not the house).
2. **Delimitor of what is to be proved:** 'Fire' (We’re talking about fire, not water).
3. **Delimitor of the reason:** 'Smokeness' (It is because of smoke that fire is achieved).

4. Types of separators:
There are two main types:
1. **Relationship separator:** One that determines a relationship (such as \- coincidence relationship and combination relationship).
2. **Dharma-determiner:** One who determines a dharma (such as \- being a pot, being a human being).

5. Conclusion:
In short, \- **"Without which property the specific knowledge of an object is not possible, that property is the 'Avacchedaka' of that object.
As stated earlier \- **"The separation of parties is not contradicted by a minor contradiction"**. This means that if the subject matter (party) of the dispute is determined even by a small (simple) religion (the separator of partisanship), then there is no contradiction.
