# First paragraph - 1. Breaking the interpretation of falsity - The thought of contradiction

<details>
  <summary>समीरवाणी</summary>


**Sameervani \-** 'Disputation' is a state of two opposing opinions (doubt or contradiction) on the same subject. It deals with whether the universe is true or false.

**nyāyāmṛtam**   
nanu mithyaiva viśvam | tathā hi tatra vipratipattiḥ- brahmapramānyena vā saprakāreṇa vā abādhyatve sati asadvilakṣaṇatve sati brahmānyat pratipannopādhau traikālikaniṣedhapratiyogi, na vā? pāramārthikatvākāreṇa traikālikaniṣedhapratiyogi na vā? akhaṇḍārthaniṣṭhavedāntajanyaniṣprakārakabrahmapramābādhyamapi viśvaṃ brahmapramānyena vā saprakāreṇa vā na bādhyamiti nāśyāsiddhiḥ | evamuttaratrāpi pūrvottarapakṣānumānapratijñe saṅkalayya vipratipattiruhyā |

**Sameervani \-** The former (Advaita) says \- "This whole world is a lie.

**Nature of Technical Doubt:**
The author describes the doubt between the Advaita and Dvaita views as follows:

* **In the case of being unbound by the Brahmanical authority:** The world is not destroyed by ordinary worldly knowledge (just as pure silver is destroyed by knowledge alone, so the world is not destroyed by knowledge alone).
* **In the case of non-distinctiveness:** The world is not 'like a rabbit's horn' (like a rabbit's horn) at all empty or alien.
* **Brahman is the competitor of the threefold prohibition in the attribute of the other, is it not? :** The question is this: \- Is the universe not in the place where it appears, even in the three times (past, future and present)?
* If **'there is'** (there is a prohibition of non-existence), then the world is **false** (Advaita).
* If **'no'** (there is no prohibition), then the world is **true** (dualism).


* According to the Advaitas, only 'Brahma-jnana' can disturb (destroy) the world, and no other knowledge. Although the world is obstructed by knowledge of the Brahman, the world is perceived in normal time, so there is nothing wrong with this idea of ​​'ashrayasiddhi' (absence of the worldly world).
* In this way, in other matters, the contradiction (two opposing views) should be known by matching the arguments and inferences of the former side (Advaita) and the latter side (Dvaita).

**In Brief \-** In this part the author explains that \- Advaitas consider the world to be 'false', because according to them, knowledge of the Brahman hinders the world. The Dvaitas (the author) believe that the world is 'true' The logical nature of these two aspects is presented here as a 'paradox'

**nyāyāmṛtam**  
idaṃ ca vipratipattipradarśanaṃ tārkikarītyaiva na tu vastutaḥ | tatpakṣe'pi vipratipatti vākyasya pakṣaparigrahekaphalakatvāt | tasya ca kathābāhyena nigrahānarheṇa laukika-rītyanusāriṇā saṃskṛtarūpeṇa vā, bhāṣārūpeṇa vā, 'mayā prapañcamithyātvaṃ sādhyate' iti vādivākyena vā, 'tvayā prapañcamithyātvaṃ sādhya'miti madhyasthaparikalpita-viṣayasvīkāreṇa vā siddhau pratijñāvyatiriktasya uktakusṛṣṭiyuktasya vipratipatti-vākyasya vaiyarthyāt | na ca sādhyopasthityarthaṃ tat | pratijñayaiva tatsiddheḥ | na ca pakṣatvaprayojakasaṃśayārthaṃ tat | vādiprativādinoḥ prāśnikānāñca niścayavattvena tadyogāt | āhāryasaṃśayasya cātiprasaṅgitvena pakṣatvāprayojakatvāt, vipratipatti-vākyānapekṣatvācca, saṃśayaṃ vinā'pi siṣādhayiṣāvirahasahakṛtasādhakamānābhāvarūpasya pakṣatvasya sambhavācca |

**Sameervani\-**

* The contradiction shown here is only in accordance with the laws of logic, it is not a real contradiction.
* Even according to the Advaitas, the main purpose of such a controversial statement is simply to make one's view acceptable.
* If the plaintiff says in general terms, in Sanskrit or any other language, 'I prove the world false,' or the arbitrator makes the matter clear, that is all that works. So there’s no point in such a tedious logical statement, it’s useless.
* This argument is not even useful for knowing the object (subject).
* Because the main promise statement alone easily identifies the object of achievement.
* This sentence is also not needed to create the doubt necessary in logic.
* The plaintiffs, defendants and arbitrators who participate in the argument of the Scriptures are all firm in their opinions, so there is no room for any doubt in their minds.
* Doubt that is artificial should not be the basis of discussion. There can be doubt even without a statement of contradiction. If the speaker has only the desire for perfection in his mind, then the scriptural argument is happily possible even without doubt.
