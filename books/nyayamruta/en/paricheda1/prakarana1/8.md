**14. nyāyāmṛta  vākyam \-**  
yadvā bādhyatvamanirvācyatvam | tacca śuktirūpyādireva pāramārthikatvākāreṇa traikālikaniṣedhapratiyogīti mate pratipannopādhau traikālikaniṣedhapratiyogitvam | uktaṃ hi vivaraṇe— “pratipannopādhāvabhāvapratiyogitvalakṣaṇasya mithyātvasya” iti | uktaṃ ca vārtike \-

tattvamasyādivākyārthasamyagdhījanmamātrataḥ |  
avidyā saha kāryeṇa nāsīdasti bhaviṣyati || iti |

pratipannatvaṃ ca na pramitatvaṃ, yena virodhaḥ | nāpi bhrāntipratipannatvam, yena siddhasādhanam | kintu pratītatvamātram | sarvasmiṃśca pratipannopādhau traikālikaniṣedhapratiyogitvaṃ vivakṣitam | sattvapakṣe rajatatvaṃ pratipannopādhau śuktau niṣidhyamānamapi rajate na niṣidhyata iti na siddhasādhanam || 2 ||

**samīravāṇī \-**

**Teacher** – Boy, let’s consider the meaning of this serious statement in pieces. The Advaitas, in explaining what **sublatability** is, say – **"or that sublatability is ineffable"**. This means that what is bound (withdrawn) by knowledge is inexpressible and false.

**Disciple** – Lord, what is the meaning of the phrase **"The competitiveness of the threefold prohibition in the attained state"**?

**Teacher** – Listen, there are three parts: **Pratipannopadhau** – Where an object is perceived (as silver appears in purity), that is its **Locus**. 2. **Triple-temporal prohibition** – The extreme absence of that object in that position, even in past, future and present tenses. 3. **Competitiveness** – That which is forbidden (such as silver), is **Counter-positive**. Therefore, by saying that **"Shukti, silver and others are the competitors of the threefold prohibition in the form of transcendence"**, the Advaitas say that the silver shining in Shukti is never actually there, only **Illusion**.

**Disciple** – Why is it said here **"in the form of transcendence"**?

**Guru** – If this is not said, then there would be an **Under pervasive** error in the characteristic because of the presence of silver there in the reflective form. Therefore, it is forbidden 'as real'

**Disciple** – Point out the ancient basis for this trait.

**Guru** – Look, the teachers have said – **"For it is said in the description— 'of the falsehood of the characteristic of the competitiveness of the being of the attained object'"**. The text of the description makes it clear that where an object is seen, its absence-competition is a sign of falsity. Thus the Advaitas forbid the objects existing in the world.

Now the Advaitas are further refining their characteristics. They quote the **Vartika** (Vartika) to embellish their views – **"The Tattva is only born with the meaning of this original statement. Ignorance with its action did not exist and will not exist.

**Disciple** – Lord, what does the word 'attainment' mean in this characteristic? If this means 'valid knowledge', then there will be a contradiction, since it is not possible to forbid a measured object.

**Teacher** – Well thought out. This is where the subtle insight of the Advaitas lies. They say – **"And the perceivedness is not the measuredness, by which there is contradiction. Nor is the perceivedness of delusion, by which it is the means of perfection. But it is only the perceivedness"**. 1. **Validly known** – This meaning is not taken, otherwise the characterization would be impossible due to contradiction. 2. **Known only through illusion** – This is not the meaning either, otherwise even in the opinion of our dualists, the error of **Proving what is already accepted) would fall because of the non-existence of the subject of illusion. 3. **Mere appearance** – Mere appearance is defined here as the object of knowledge.

**Disciple** – Then why is it that **"and in all the attained titles the competitiveness of the threefold prohibition is defined"**?

**Teacher** – This is the main point. If the prohibition of only one **locus** is mentioned, then the prohibition of silver in purity is desirable even on our dualistic side. But Advaita says that wherever an object is perceived (just as silver is perceived in silver), there is also a prohibition on it. **"It is not a perfect means that even what is forbidden in the pure state of being silver on the side of Sattva is not forbidden in silver"**.

**Disciple** – This means very strange \! Are they saying that there is no silver in silver either?

**Teacher** – Yes, but according to them, **sublatability** is such that there is nothing anywhere from a transcendental point of view. This allows them to avoid the **perfect means** error.

15. nyāyāmṛta  vākyam \- matāntare tu bādhyatvaṃ jñānatvena jñānanivartyatvam | uktaṃ hi vivaraṇe- "ajñānasya svakāryyeṇa varttamānena pravilīnena vā saha jñānena nivṛttirbādhaḥ" iti | anumiti prati manaso manastvena hetutve'pīndriyatvenāhetutvavat pūrvajñānādikaṃ prati uttarasya jñānecchāderuttarātmaviśeṣaguṇatvena nivarttakatve'pi jñānatvenānivarttakatvāt || 3 ||

**samīravāṇī \-**

**Teacher** – Boy, now we’re going to consider the difference in characteristics according to that. Here the Advaitas define **obligation** (Sublatability) in another way – **"but in another view, obligation is knowledge-reversible as knowledge"**. This means that 'knowledge' is the knowledge rewarded by religion, and that which is restrained by it is obligation.

**Disciple** – Lord, what does this mean? If the latter knowledge in the world destroys the former knowledge, then are all the objects of knowledge false?

**Teacher** – Not at all. The term "as knowledge" is very important here. The Advaitas take the basis of the text **Vivarana** – **"For it is said in the Vivarana: 'The renunciation of ignorance by knowledge, whether present or merged with its own action, is an obstacle'"**. Ignorance is destroyed by knowledge along with its own actions (the world), and that is the obstacle.

**Disciple** – What is the fruit of the investment 'as knowledge'?

**Teacher** – Listen, there is an example given – **"As the mind is the cause of the mind towards inference, even as the senses are the cause of the senses"**. For example, in inferential knowledge, the mind is the cause in the form of 'mindfulness', not in the form of 'sensoryness' Similarly, here – **"since the desire for knowledge of the latter towards previous knowledge and other things is deterrent as a special quality of the latter self, but also as a deterrent as knowledge"**.

In the world, later knowledge destroys former knowledge, but it destroys it not 'as knowledge', but 'as a subsequent quality of the self' According to Advaita, however, knowledge of the Absolute destroys ignorance as 'knowledge' Therefore, even in the mutual destruction of knowledge in the world, there is no **overlap** error.

**Disciple** – Oh subtle analysis\! Thus, then, the Advaitas consider the renunciation of ignorance to be the transcendental **obligation** (Sublatability).

**Teacher** – True.

**Disciple** – Lord, how does this destruction arise when **"the latter knowledge in the world destroys the former knowledge"**? What is the reason for that?

**Guru** – This idea that **"Post knowledge in the world destroys previous knowledge"** is mainly known in **Logical Philosophy** (Nyaya-Vaisheshika Philosophy). According to the logical view, knowledge exists as a 'self-specific quality' and is transient.

According to the logicians, the Special qualities of all-pervading substances are destroyed by the self-sufficient quality. Therefore, **Subsequent knowledge** destroys the **Previous knowledge** of its predecessor. This is what is called 'the destruction of the former by the latter'

**Disciple** – So why do the Advaitas bring this up here?

**Guru** – Advaitas mention this here to avoid the error of **Over pervasive**. If they were to say only that 'knowledge is inevitable and falsity', then, according to the logical view, prior knowledge is also inevitable to later knowledge, and therefore there would be a case of falsity of prior knowledge.

**Disciple** – So how do they resolve it?

**Teacher** – They say that according to the logical view, later knowledge destroys former knowledge **"as a subsequent quality of the soul"**, not **"as knowledge"**. According to Advaita, however, the destruction of ignorance by realizing the **Locus** is by the Dharma of 'knowledge' This is the difference between the logical view and the Advaita view.

**Disciple** – Now it’s clear. According to the logicians, destruction is a natural process, but according to Advaita, it is a sublatation.

**Teacher** – True. Thus, by using the process of the Supreme Being, the Advaitas try to exonerate their own **badhyatvam** (Sublatability).

**Guru** – Calf, **"Since the desire for knowledge of the latter towards previous knowledge and others is deterrent as a special quality of the latter self, but also as deterrent as knowledge" \-** This argument of the Advaitas is very subtle. They use logical opinion (Nyaya logic) to support their characteristics. Listen to the counterpart of this sentence –

**"Towards prior knowledge and other things"** – The goal here is **Previous knowledge**. Logically, when new knowledge is born, old knowledge is destroyed. Therefore, the destroyer actually has this prior knowledge of the 'competitor'

**"The desire for knowledge of the latter"** – Here the term **subsequent** refers to the subsequent substance. The term **knowledge and desire** means 'next knowledge' or 'desire' Logically, the qualities of the self (such as knowledge, desire, and effort) are gradually born.

**Disciple** – Lord, what is the relationship between these?

**Guru** – **"as a special quality of the latter self"** – This is the main reason. The term **subsequent special quality of the soul** means that the new quality (knowledge of the soul) which has arisen in the soul destroys its previous quality. Logically, it is the rule that the 'special qualities of the Almighty' are destroyed by the latter.
**"Even though it acts as a destroyer"** – This means 'even though it acts as a destroyer' That is, the latter knowledge is the deterrent (destroyer) of the former knowledge, but in what form?

**Disciple** – Yeah, that’s what the Advaitas explain, right?

**The Guru** – True, **"because it is inevitable as knowledge"** – here the phrase 'because it is non-inevitable' That is, the latter knowledge does not destroy the former knowledge **as knowledge** (In the capacity of being 'knowledge'), but only as 'the latter quality' If it destroys the latter knowledge 'as knowledge', then where 'desire' arises, there is no destruction of the former knowledge, since desire is not knowledge.

**Disciple** – Oh \! Therefore, the Advaitas say that the sublatation of ignorance is by 'knowledge' Dharma, which is not seen in the world.

**Teacher** – Well understood. By doing so, they prevent the **Over pervasive** error. According to them, knowledge of the Absolute is as destructive to ignorance as light is to darkness, not as one knowledge is to another.

**Disciple** – This proves the **Sublatability** of the Advaita doctrine to be very remarkable.

**Teacher** – True.

16. nyāyāmṛta  vākyam \-      
athavā citsukhirītyā svasamānādhikaraṇātyantābhāvapratiyogitvaṃ mithyātvam | saṃyogatadatyantābhāvayoravacchedakabhedena bhinnāśritatvasyaivānubhavena sattvapakṣe sāmānādhikaraṇyābhāvāt | śuktirūpyāderapi pāramārthikatvākāreṇa svasamānādhi-karaṇātyantābhāvapratiyogitvācca | na cādhikaraṇaśabdena tāttvikādhikaraṇoktau samavāyini saṃyogini vā tattvatastadadhikaraṇe tadatyantābhāvāyogenāsambhavaḥ | adhikaraṇatvena pratītatvamātroktau cānyathākhyātipakṣe rajatatvādeḥ svādhikaraṇatvena pratītaśuktyādāvatyantābhāvasya sattvātsiddhasādhanamiti vācyam, svātyantābhāvādhikaraṇa eva pratīyamānatvasya vivakṣitatvāt | tatpakṣe ca rajatatvādikaṃ svātyantābhāvādhikaraṇādhikaraṇe rajate'pi bhāti || 4 ||

**samīravāṇī \-**

**Teacher** – Boy, now we are going to consider the fourth characteristic of falsehood, which is supported by the Chitsukhacharyas. The Advaitas say – **"Or, in the manner of Chitsukhi, the competition of the extreme absence of self-equality is falsehood"**. This means that where an object is perceived (self-equivalence), there is its extreme absence, and its object is **counter-positive**. Listen, there are two parts here

**Extreme absence of self-equal authority** – **Same locus or **place** where **self** (silver) is perceived. The **Absolute negation** existing in the same authority (Shukta).

**From competitiveness** – The **Counter-positive or object of negation) of that absence is called **false**.

**Disciple** – Lord, wouldn’t this symptom be **Over pervasive**? such as Contact. In a tree, there is a connection in the branch, but not in the root. Therefore, union has become a competitor of the extreme absence of its equal authority. Is the coincidence false?

**Guru** – The Advaitas say here – **"Since there is no equal authority on the side of Sattva by the experience of different dependence by the distinctive difference between union and extreme absence"**. This means that in a tree, union is in the region called 'branch' and absence is in the region called 'root' Because of the difference in country, they do not really have the same authority (Being in the same locus). But in the same separation of perception of a false object (such as pure silver), there is also absence.

**Disciple** – Then why is it said **"even in the form of transcendence of purity, silver and other things, and because of their equal authority and competition of extreme absence"**?

**Guru:** –

**"Silver appearing in mother-of-pearl etc."** – Here the term **silver appearing in mother-of-pearl etc.) refers to the subjects of confusion. Here the word 'adi' means all false objects such as rope and snake.

**"In the form of transcendence"** – This group of terms is very important. It means 'in the capacity of being absolutely real' According to the Advaitas, silver in purity exists in its 'apparent' form, however, and therefore it is not possible to forbid it at all. But there is never silver there as 'absolute reality', so they add the adjective 'in the form of transcendence'

**Extreme absence of self-equal authority** – **Same locus or **place** where **self** (silver) is perceived. The **Absolute negation** existing in the same authority (Shukta).

**From competitiveness** – The **Counter-positive or object of negation) of that absence is called **false**.

**Disciple** – So what is the conclusion of this sentence?

**Guru** – The conclusion is that the prohibition that silver perceived in the time of **Illusion** is not in the form of 'truth' in the same authority has the objectivity in silver. Thus, in order to protect their characteristic of falsehood from the error of **over-pervasiveness, the Advaitas say that it is 'in the form of transcendence', which would prohibit only truthfulness, not perception.

The Advaitas resolve the doubt by raising it to refine their own characteristics \- **And it is not possible for the word authority to be used in the statement of the Tattvika authority in the compound or conjunctive in fact by the combination of its extreme absence**. Listen to the profound meaning of this sentence –

**"Not by the word authority in the statement of the Tattvika authority"** – The Advaita doubts that, if in this characteristic the word 'Adhikarana' (Locus) is taken to mean **Tattvikadhikarana** (Real or Absolute Locus), then what is the harm? Here, **philosophical** means what actually exists.

**Disciple** – Lord, if the true authority is accepted, what is the fault?

**Guru** – There they say – **"in the compound or in the conjunctive in fact in the authority of that"**. Two relationships are implied here: **Compound** – As the fabric remains in compound relationship with the fibers. 2. **Sanyogini** – Just as a pot stands on the ground in a relationship of union. Logically, the fibers of the cloth and the ground are the 'essence' (object) of the pot.

**"That is impossible by the combination of the absence of the extreme"** – If the fibers are the real basis of the fabric, then there is never the **Absolute negation** of the fabric. Where an object actually exists, it cannot be said that it does not exist in all three times. Therefore, if the authorization is 'Real', then the **Impossibility** error of the characteristic of falsity would fall, since the characteristic would not occur anywhere.

**Disciple** – Oh, then what should the authority be like according to the Advaitas?

**Guru** – According to them, **Locus** is defined only as **Appearing as a locus**, not the transcendental being. They say that if we accept real authority, then the **sublatability** of the Advaita doctrine itself cannot be achieved.

**Disciple** – So then, according to them, Brahman is the only Tattva authority?

**Teacher** – True.

How the Advaitas forbid the theory of **Anyatha-khyati** (Anyatha-khyati) to refine their own characteristics, is to be said to be a perfect means in the case of **”Anyatha-khyati only in the statement of perception as an authority..."** \- By a step-by-step analysis of this sentence.

The subject of the Theory of Error is very profound, and therefore a discussion is already necessary. Without fame, the characteristics of falsity are not fully understood. Just as to know medicine one must know the nature of disease (illusion), so to know knowledge one must know the process of ignorance (fame).

**Disciple** – Oh, this discussion will be very enlightening. I am ready for this.

**Guru** – Calf, **Theory of Error** is a great topic in Indian philosophies to determine the nature of knowledge of error. There are five principal fame, which Advaitas and other philosophers use to confirm their respective positions. Listen, I'll compile them and tell you in detail.

**1. Atma-khyati** is accepted by the **Yoga Buddhists**. According to them, there is no external world, only 'science' (Consciousness). Therefore, in **Illusion** time, the knowledge within appears to be outside. For example, the silver on the inside is perceived on the outside.

**2. Asat-khyati** This is accepted by the **secondary Buddhists** (the nihilists). According to these, what appears (such as silver in purity), is completely 'non-existent' The void itself appears as an object because of delusion.

**3. Akhyati** This is accepted by the **Prabhakara-mystics**. According to these, delusion is nothing separate. There are two knowledges here—the 'Perception' of the pearl and the 'Memory' of the silver. Man acts from the Non-apprehension of difference between them. They argue that there is no **under-pervasive** error.

**4. Anyatha-khyati** This is accepted by the **logical (Nyaya)**. This means—otherwise (in another way) fame. **Logically** – Silver that shines in purity is 'true' elsewhere (in itself). That **Locus** illuminates the pearl as 'silver'

**5. Anirvachaniya-khyati** This is the fame of the **Advaitas** themselves. Here silver is neither 'true' (because of obstacle), nor 'false' (because of perception). Therefore, they describe **Sublatability** as being 'Indefinable'

**Disciple** – Lord, how is **logical** fame different from Advaita?

**Teacher** – According to the logicians, fame is 'about good', because the silver is elsewhere. The Advaita, however, says that **"since perceivability is defined as the authority of the absence of self-existence"**. According to them, even where it seems (even in silver), it is lacking. Thus they achieve in the absence of **competitiveness** (Being a counter-positive) of all things.

**Disciple** – Oh\! Thus the distinction between the three philosophies (Buddhist, logical and Advaita) is made clear by the arguments of fame.

**Teacher** – True. The logicians say 'Sat', the Buddhists say 'Asat', while the Advaitas say 'Different from sat and asat'

**Advaiti** says here—**"And in the case of the mere statement of perception as authority, it can be said that the absence of the self-authority of silver and others as the authority of silver and others is the perfect means The lack of silver is the same according to us and the logicians.

**"And in the mere statement of perception as an authority"** – the Advaita presents the former side (doubt). If the name 'Locus' is not true, but only 'that which appears as an authority' (**only appearance**), then what is the problem?

**"On the side of otherwise known as silver and so on"** – The opinion of the logicians is called **otherwise known**. In this aspect, the silver that appears in the pure is actually the true silver that exists elsewhere.

**"Because of the existence of the absence of the ultimate from the perceived pearl as self-authority"** – In our opinion, even pearl is perceived as the authority of silver, but there is still the **Absolute negation** of silver.

**"It is to be said that it is a perfect means"** – Here the doubt is over. If the Advaita says that 'absence is falsehood where it is perceived', then the Dvaitas say the same thing (that there is no silver in purity). If this is done, the efforts of the Advaitas are in vain, for they accomplish what we have already rejected. This is the **proving means** (Proving the already proven) error.

**Disciple** – Lord, then how does the Advaita answer?

**Guru** – The solution to that is very clever – **"since the authority of the absence of independence is the definition of perceivability"**. Advaiti says: We don't just say 'there is no silver in purity' Rather, our definition is that where an object is perceived, it is absent.

**"And on the other hand, the silverness and other things appear even in silver in the authority of the authority of the absence of independence"** – This is the last blow of Advaita. According to the dualism, there is no **silverness** in pureness, but there is **silvery** in 'silver' (In real silver). The Advaita, however, says, 'Even in silver, there is no **silverness** transcendental. Wherever silver is perceived (whether in pure or silver), there is **three-time prohibition competition** everywhere. An object whose locus never has existence in the past, future and present tenses is said to have the competitiveness of three-time prohibition there. Thus they avoid the **perfect means** error.

This word **'in the authority of the absence of independence'** is very serious and terminological. Let us examine the meaning of this paragraph, which will make clear the refined characteristics of the Advaitas.

The conflict of this is thus – **the **extreme absence** of **itself** (silver), its **authority** (Locus), that is the **authority** of which, in.

**Negation of itself:** Here the word 'self' means **Silver-ness** or silver. The Absolute negation of the Trinity.

**Locus of that negation:** Where he lacks silver. According to the logicians, pearl is the authority of the extreme absence of silver.

**Locus as the appearing locus:** This part is the main one. Advaiti says where does silver shine? In the authority (pureness) of the same extreme absence, it appears as the authority.

The Advaita says – **"The silverness and other things appear even in silver in the authority of the authority of the absence of the independent"**. This means –

* In our opinion, there is no silver in purity, but there is in silver.
* Advaita says that even in **true silver**, 'silver-ness' is not transcendental.
* Therefore, even true silver is the **extreme absence authority** of its (silverness), and in that authority it appears to be silver.

**Disciple** – Oh \! Thus, then, according to them, the whole universe is perceived in the authority of the absence of independence.

**Teacher** – True. This is their **Illusion** process. This makes their **obligation** (Sublatability) universal.

17. nyāyāmṛta  vākyam \-    yadvā''nandabodhoktarītyā sadviviktatvaṃ mithyātvam | tacca sadrūpatvābhāvaḥ | brahma ca sattārahitamapi sāmānyamiva sadrūpam || 5 ||

**guruḥ** – vatsa, idānīṃ vayaṃ **mithyātvasya** pañcamam lakṣaṇaṃ paśyāmaḥ, yat ācārya-ānandabodhaiḥ (Anandabodha) proktam | etat lakṣaṇaṃ pūrvalakṣaṇebhyaḥ saralam iva pratīyate, paraṃ atīva gambhīram asti |

**"yadvā''nandabodhoktarītyā sadviviktatvaṃ mithyātvam"** atra **sadviviktatvam** (Being distinct from the Real) iti mithyātvasya lakṣaṇam | 'vivikta' ityasya arthaḥ 'bhinna' athavā 'pṛthak' | yat 'sat' padārthāt bhinnaṃ tat 'mithyā' |

**"tacca sadrūpatvābhāvaḥ"** asya pariṣkāraṃ kurvan ānandabodhaḥ vadati yat sadviviktatvaṃ nāma **sadrūpatvābhāvaḥ** (Absence of the nature of reality) | arthāt yasmin 'sat' iti dharmaḥ nāsti, tat mithyā |

**śiṣyaḥ** – bhagavan, atra tu mahatī śaṅkā jāyate \! yadi 'sadrūpatvābhāvaḥ' (Absence of Reality) mithyātvaṃ, tarhi **brahma** api mithyā bhavet | kutaḥ? yataḥ brahmaṇi 'sattā' iti jātiḥ nāsti (brahma nirguṇaṃ nirākāraṃ ca) |

**guruḥ** – sādhu vatsa \! etasyaiva samādhānaṃ agrime vākye asti – **"brahma ca sattārahitamapi sāmānyamiva sadrūpam"** |

1. **sattārahitamapi** – tārkikamate 'sattā' (Existence) ekā jātiḥ asti, yā dravya-guṇa-karmasu tiṣṭhati | brahma tu jāti-rahitam, ataḥ tat 'sattārahitam' ucyate |   
2. **sāmānyamiva** – yathā 'sāmānyam' (Universals) svayaṃ sattā-rahitaṃ sat api 'sat' rūpeṇa gṛhyate, tathā brahma asti |   
3. **sadrūpam** – brahma svayaṃ **'sat'** svarūpameva asti, ataḥ tasmin 'sadrūpatvābhāvaḥ' nāsti |

 vatsa, atra advaitināṃ kaścana atīva sūkṣmaḥ tarkaḥ asti | etaṃ viṣayaṃ boddhuṃ tārkikamate (Nyaya) 'sattā' iti padārthasya jñānaṃ prathamam āvaśyakam |

**śiṣyaḥ** – bhagavan, tārkikāḥ **'sattā'** iti kāṃ vadanti ?

**guruḥ** – śṛṇu, tārkikamate **'sattā'** ekā **jātiḥ** (Universal) asti, yā dravya-guṇa-karmasu samavāyasambandhena tiṣṭhati | yathā ghaṭe 'ghaṭatvaṃ' bhavati, tathā sarvasmin vidyamāne vastuni 'sattā' bhavati | idānīṃ advaitināṃ vākyaṃ khaṇḍaśaḥ paśyāmaḥ \-

 

**ब्रह्म च सत्तारहितमपि (Brahman is without 'Satta')**  
अद्वैतमते ब्रह्म **निर्गुणं** तथा **निर्विशेषं** अस्ति । यदि ब्रह्मणि 'सत्ता' इति जातिः अङ्गीक्रियते, तर्हि ब्रह्म 'सजातीय-भेद' युक्तं भवेत् (अर्थात् 'सत्ता' इति धर्मः अन्यः, ब्रह्म च अन्यत्) । अतः अद्वैतिनः वदन्ति यत् ब्रह्मणि तार्किक-सम्मता 'सत्ता-जातिः' नास्ति ।

**सामान्यमिव (Like Universals/Generality)**  
तार्किकमते एकः नियमः अस्ति – **"जातिषु जातिः न तिष्ठति"** (No universal exists in another universal) ।

* यथा 'मनुष्यत्व' इति जातौ अन्या 'सत्ता' इति जातिः न भवति ।  
* तथापि वयं 'मनुष्यत्वं सत्' (Human-ness is real) इति वदामः ।  
* किमर्थम् ? यतः सा जातिः स्वयं 'सद्रूपा' (Real in nature) अस्ति, तस्याः कृते अन्या 'सत्ता' न अपेक्षिता ।

 

**सद्रूपम् (Nature of Existence itself)**  
एवमेव ब्रह्मणि 'सत्ता' इति जातिः नास्ति चेदपि, ब्रह्म **स्वयं सत्-स्वरूपम्** अस्ति । यथा दीपः अन्यं प्रकाशयितुं अन्यं दीपं न अपेक्षते (स्वयंप्रकाशत्वात्), तथा ब्रह्म स्वयं 'सत्' अस्ति, अतः तस्मिन् 'सद्रूपत्वाभावः' (Absence of reality) नास्ति ।

**शिष्यः** – अहो \! एवं तर्हि **मिथ्यात्व-लक्षणम्** (सद्विविक्तत्वं / सद्रूपत्वाभावः) ब्रह्मणि न घटते, यतः ब्रह्म स्वयं 'सत्' अस्ति । परं जगत् तु 'सत्-स्वरूपात्' भिन्नं वर्तते, अतः तत् मिथ्या ।

**गुरुः** – सत्यम् \! अत्र अद्वैती 'सत्ता-जाति' (Categorical existence) तथा 'सद्रूपता' (Essential existence) इत्यनयोः भेदं कृत्वा स्वलक्षणं समर्थयति । एतेन **अव्याप्ति** (Under-pervasive) दोषः निवृत्तः भवति ।
