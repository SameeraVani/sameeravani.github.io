# First paragraph - 1. Breaking the interpretation of falsity - The inferences made by others in falsity - Part -

<details>
  <summary>समीरवाणी</summary>


**nyāyāmṛtam**  
1. yadvā, pṛthivī satyā mithyā vā, jalaṃ satyaṃ mithyā vetyananugatadharmyāśrayā anugatā vipratipattiḥ pakṣatāvacchedikā | 2. yadyapi vimatissamayabandhādinā vyavahitatvānnānumānakāle'sti, tathāpi saivopalakṣaṇatayā tajjñānaṃ vā viśeṣaṇatayā pakṣatāvacchedakamastu | 3. ata eva vimatamityeva prācāṃ prayogaḥ | 4. na tu viyadādītyeva pakṣanirdeśaḥ | 5. avacchedakābhāve'saṅkucitenādiśabdena ātmāderapi grahaṇaprasaṅgāt | 6. nāpi prapañca ityeva pakṣanirdeśaḥ | 7. prapañcaśabdena viyadādyanyatamatatsamudāyoktau viyadādimithyātvāsiddheḥ | 8. viyadādīnāmevoktāvavacchedakābhāvenoktadoṣāt | 9. nāpi viyadityeva pakṣanirdeśaḥ, ghaṭādistu pakṣatulya iti vācyam |  10. pakṣādanyatra niścitasādhyābhāvavati hetusandeha iva niścitahetumati sādhyasandehe'pi sandigdhānaikāntyāt | 11. pratijñāṃ pratyaviṣaye ghaṭe svārthānumānānugatasya sādhakabādhakapramāṇaviraharūpapakṣatvasya sattve'pi evaṃvidhapakṣanirdeśasya ghaṭādisādhāraṇavipratipattyānānuguṇyācca | 12. vipratipattau tu na vimatirdharmitāvacchedikā, ātmāśrayāt |

**Sameervani\-**
1. Or, there is a debate about whether the earth is true or false, and whether water is true or false. Among these different objects (earth, water, etc.), there is some one common dispute (vipratipati), and that alone characterizes (deduces) the party (subject of dispute).

2. Although that previous dispute is not directly present at the time of consideration, it is the knowledge of that dispute or that contradiction that becomes the attribute of the party and makes it certain. This line is about the **'party separator'** (characteristic of the party).

* **'by time constraint etc.'** **Time** \= Rule/Convention or Time. **Binding** \= limit, determination (Binding/Agreement). **etc.** \= other reasons (etc.). The main meaning of this term is \- **"Rules or constraints fixed for a debate/process.**
* In a debate or controversy, there are certain **rules** between the plaintiff and the defendant. Those rules are called 'time bonds'
For example, the rule "In this time the dispute (contradiction) will end" or "The sight of a certain proof should not cause doubt.
* **Context in the sentence \-**
When we speculate, there is 'Doubt or Disagreement' before it. But when the time comes for speculation, that disagreement does not remain. Why doesn't it stay? There could be two reasons \-
* **Because of the time limit \-** That disagreement has already been terminated by some rule.
* **A lot of time has passed since it was determined, so that disagreement is now lost.
* **When does a disagreement (dispute) occur?** \- In the meaning of the scriptures, a 'dispute' (dispute) between the plaintiff and the defendant occurs before the inference (argument) begins.
* **Lack of inference:** \- When we make inferences (arguments), that disagreement is not realized because it is removed by a time gap.
* **Problem :** \- According to jurisprudence, the religion that specifies the party (determining the party), must be present at the time of inference. But the disagreement has already ended.
* **Solution (1) \- As a sub-characteristic :** \- Although the disagreement does not exist now, it is an **'sub-characteristic'** (Indicator) and indicates the aspect. For example, in \- "The house given to God like a crow," the crow does not always stay at home, yet it signifies the house.
* **Solution (2) \- Knowledge of that dispute as an adjective :** \- Even if that dispute is not directly present, **'Knowledge of that dispute'** is still present in our intellect at the time of inference.
* **Determinism of aspect:** \- Therefore, that disagreement (as a sub-characteristic) or its knowledge (as an adjective) must be a characteristic of the party (as a determining factor).
* **Conclusion:** \- This proves that 'Disputedness' is the religion of the party in the inference "Disputed is false...", even if the actual dispute has already occurred.

 **विशेषणम्** तथा **उपलक्षणम्** 

In logic, there is a big difference between the two words **adjective** and **subcharacteristic**. Both are used to distinguish/identify an object, but their positions are different.

Here is a simple distinction provided:

**1. Adjective (Visheshana \- Attribute/Qualitative Property)**

A religion that is **present (Present)** with an object, and thus distinguishes it, is called an 'attribute'

* **Characteristic:** Present in the working time when it is invertible. (That which distinguishes AND is present during the action.)
* **Example:** "Blue Lotus"
* Here is the adjective 'blue-color' Because when we see a lotus, the blue color remains within that lotus. The color is associated with the appearance of the lotus.

**2. Subcharacteristic (Upalakshana \- Accidental Indicator)**

A religion that **does not always remain (Not always present)** with an object, but only identifies that object, is called a 'sub-characteristic'

* **Symptom:** Absence during operation in the case of reversibility. (That which distinguishes but is NOT present during the action.)
* **Example:** "Devadatta's house where a crow is sitting.
* Someone asks \- "Where is Devadatta's house?"
* The answer is found \- "Where that crow sat, that is the house of the god.
* There is a 'crow' subcharacteristic here. If the crow flies away, the house has already been identified. A crow doesn’t always need to be positioned there to identify a home.

**3. In the context of your sentence**

Your sentence discusses that \- **"Disagreement"** does not exist in estimation.

1. If we consider disagreement to be an **'attribute'**, then that disagreement should be inevitable at the time of inference (but it is not).
2. Therefore, the author says that the disagreement should be **'subcharacteristically'**. That is, just as a crow tells the house (even if he is not there), so 'past disagreement' will tell the present guess.

3. That is why the ancient teachers use the word "vimatam" (i.e., the matter on which there is dispute).
4. They do not use the word "viyadadi" (sky-etc.) as an aspect.
5. If there is no definite dharma (deductive), then the word "beginning" may also include the eternal Self (Brahman), which is not desirable.
6. Even the word "world" (world) is not appropriate to use as an aspect indication.
7. Because if the word "universe" refers to a specific group, it does not prove that every thing, such as the sky, is false separately.
8. If it is only called "the sky-and-others," then the aforementioned error (grasping the soul) comes back.
9. Nor should it be said, "Only the sky is the wing, but the pot is like it (equal to the wing).
10. Because if there is a cause in the pot but there is doubt about the object, then an error of reasoning called "doubtful solitude" arises. The implication of this line is about **side assignment** (what to take as a side).

* **Proposal:** Some say that only **'viyat' (sky)** should be the aspect in the use of inference. Other objects, such as **'pots and others'**, however, are wing-like, and therefore do not need to be specified separately.
* **Disclaimer:** Such an assignment of parties is incorrect. If we only side with 'Viyat', then there will be a fallacy of **'doubtful singularity'** in pots and other things.
* **Nature of Error:** The rule of logic is that where there is a Reason, there should be no doubt about the Result (outside the party).
* Here the reason for 'perceptibility' is in the pot.
* If the pot is not a party, then there remains doubt as to whether the pot has 'falsehood' (achievability).
* In this case, that cause (visibility) is Inconclusive.
* **Analogy:** For example, where there is no definite object to be achieved (Vipaksha), if there is doubt about the cause, then it is a fault. Similarly, **where the cause is certain** (the visibility of the pot is certain), if there is **doubt of the object**, the inference is weakened.
* **Conclusion:** Therefore, the aspect of 'viyat' (sky) alone should not be accepted. Instead, the term **'disputed world'** should be used to collect all things as parties, so that there is no doubt about the pot and the like.


11. and because the indication of such an aspect is in accordance with the general contradiction of the pot and the like, even in the presence of the aspect in the form of separation of the seeker-obstacle proof following the inference of self-interest in the object of the promise

Although the pot also has the characteristics of being an aspect (lack of cause-and-effect evidence), the direction of "viyat" alone is not appropriate for all things.

* **In the case of a promise:** A pot that is not the subject of a 'promise' (Formal Statement/Thesis). (In a jar which is not the subject of the current debate).
* **Following self-inference:** What pervades inference for oneself.
* **Aspectism in the form of separation of cause and effect:** One of the characteristics of aspect is \- "the absence of proof for and proof against"
* **Even in existence:** Even in existence (Even if it exists).
* **Of such party designation:** Mention of such party.
* **And in accordance with the general disagreement of the pot and others:** Because there is no general 'Disagreement/Dispute' in the pot.


Here he shows the difference between **'self-interest inference'** and **'other-interest inference'** (the meaning of the scriptures).

1. **Bias in self-interested inference:** When we make inferences for ourselves, bias simply means \- "lack of evidence for and against" That is, if we don’t have any evidence (Neither proof for, nor proof against), then we can speculate.
     
* For example \- there is a pot. We don’t know if there’s a fire or not (No proof either way). Therefore, we can make self-interested inferences. Here the 'pot' can be the aspect.
       
2. **Problems in inferring the meaning of others:** But when we debate the meaning of the scriptures, a 'promise' is necessary. We promise the very thing, on which there is a **'Disagreement'** between the plaintiff and the defendant.
     
* If we only consider the "absence of proof" to be a party, then during a scriptural debate a man would suddenly promise that "the pot is on fire.
       
* But this is inappropriate. why? Because there is **no disagreement with the pot** in that scriptural meaning. The contradiction is to the 'mountain'
     

The point of this line is that the characteristic of \- **"Absence of proofs"** runs in self-interest, but it is not enough in debate. Because such a characteristic is also present in the 'pot', but the 'subjecthood' of the pot cannot be accepted in the absence of contradiction in the meaning of the scriptures.

Although there is no certainty of the object (such as fire) in the pot, we do not side with the pot for the purpose of the scriptures, since there is no dispute about the pot. Therefore, 'contradiction' should also be considered in the characterization of partiality.

12. In the case of contradiction, however, disagreement is not a separator of religion, since it depends on the self.

* **Contradiction:** A conflicting determination between the plaintiff and the defendant (e.g., one believes that 'word is eternal', another believes that 'word is infinite').
* **Disagreement:** Doubt or disagreement.
* **Dharmitavachchedika:** Introducing the Subject. For example, in "The mountain is fiery," 'being a mountain' is a religious separator.
* **Self-dependency:** Self-dependency/Circularity. This is some logic error.

In the time of contradiction, that contradiction itself cannot be the characteristic (the deductive of religion), otherwise there will be an error of reasoning called "self-reliance. In the time of contradiction (dispute-statement) 'vimati' (dispute) alone should not be the characteristic of the subject.

* **The fallacy of Self-dependence or Process of Circularity** **:** If we say \- "In this dispute, 'dispute' is the only characteristic", then there is **self-dependence**.
* Disagreement (controversy) depends on a subject (religious). (Controversy depends on the subject).
* If that subject is also proved by relying on disagreement (dispute), then it means that the dispute **proves itself**. (Subject depends on the same controversy).
* Here the **'controversy'** is dependent on the subject matter, and the **'subject matter'** is dependent on the dispute. This is **'self-reliance'**.
* **Conclusion:** Therefore, the characteristic of 'disagreement' does not exist in the statement of contradiction (dispute statement). But when the dispute is over, and then **'During inference'** we can accept the characteristic of 'dissent' (being the subject of dispute), there is nothing wrong with that.
