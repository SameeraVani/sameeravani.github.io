**6. nyāyāmṛta  vākyam \- nāpi bhrāntiviṣayatvam, brahmaṇo'pyadhiṣṭhānatvena tadviṣayatvāt | bhrāntimātraviṣayatvasya coktarītyā śuktirūpyādāvapyabhāvāt | adhyastatayā bhrāntiviṣayatvaṃ ced, viśeṣyavaiyarthyāt | viśeṣaṇamātrasya ca nirucyamānamithyātvānatirekāt |** 

**samīravāṇī \-**   
**Disciple —** Master\! In the Advaita doctrine, it is called 'the universe is false' What is the sixth aspect in the argument of falsity there? How is that aspect discussed from the point of view of Advaita?

**Teacher —** Well asked, son. The sixth aspect in the Advaita texts is presented as follows — **"bhrānti-viṣayatvaṃ mithyātvam"**. That is, their primary characteristic is that what is perceived as error is false.

**Disciple —** What is the inconsistency here? The symptoms, however, seem to be accurate.

**Guru:** No, the Advaitas see a great flaw here. Their idea is as follows —
**"Nor is it the object of delusion, since it is the object of Brahman as its locus."**

**The Law of Locus —** According to Advaita, when one sees an illusion (such as silver in a pearl), the knowledge of illusion there does not only take the imaginary silver, but also objectifies the **Locus** that underlies it. In the knowledge of 'this is silver', the **Locus** vibrates in the part of 'this'

**Over pervasive error —** If "being the object of delusion" were false, then **Brahma** would also be 'false' why? Because in the illusion of the world, Brahman is the main **Locus**. When one sees the world, then even the true Brahman there becomes the object of delusion. Even where the characteristic should not go (in the true Brahman), the going of the characteristic is called **Over pervasive**.

**Under pervasive thinking —** If this characteristic does not fully understand any worldly object, then there is also a possibility of an **Under pervasive** error. But here it is mainly **Over pervasive** that is shown as the error, so that the truth of the Brahman may be preserved.

**Disciple —** Oh\! So the Advaitas themselves refute this characteristic?

**Teacher —** My son, the teachers in the Advaita doctrine have seriously discussed this characteristic. Note this line there — **"bhrāntimātraviṣayatvasya coktarītyā śuktirūpyādāvapyabhāvāt."**

This means that if "being only the object of error" were false, then this would not happen even in purity and silver. Because the locus also appears in the time of delusion. Therefore, there is nowhere pure delusional subjectivity. In this case, the **Under pervasive** error would apply here.

**Disciple —** So if we refine the characteristics? If "adhyastataya bhrānti-viṣayatvaṃ mithyātvam" (being the object of error by being superimposed)?

**Teacher —** There the teacher says — **"adhyastatayā bhrāntiviṣayatvaṃ ced, viśeṣyavaiyarthyāt."**
Here the adjective "being the object of delusion" is useless. Because the basic principle of the Advaitas is that "being superimposed" is falsehood. So just "being present" is enough.

**Disciple —** If only the adjective is taken, what is the harm?

**The teacher —** there is high — **"viśeṣaṇamātrasya ca nirucyamānamithyātvānatirekāt."**
That is, if "subordination" is falsehood, then that is what we will achieve in the future. This is not a characteristic, but an achievable one. Thus the Advaitas will reject the other characteristics and finally establish the flawless characteristic in the form of competition of extreme absence, which is fixed on the **Locus**.

**Disciple —** Oh\! This characteristic is also flawed in the Advaita manner. So here the case of **Over pervasive** is also about the locus, right?

**Teacher —** True. If the characteristic goes even to the true Brahman, then it is **Over pervasive**. Therefore, avoiding all these defects, they will speak of their own characteristics.

**7. nyāyāmṛta  vākyam  \- nāpi bādhyatvam | taddhi na tāvadanyathā vijñātasya samyagvijjñātatvam | mithyātvakṣaṇikatvādinā vijñātasya prapañcasya satyatvasthāyitvādinā vijñātatvena siddhasādhanāt |**

**samīravāṇī \-** 

**Disciple —** Guru, the Advaitas say that the seventh characteristic of falsehood is 'sublatability' **"nāpi bādhyatvam. taddhi na tāvadanyathā vijñātasya samyagvijjñātatvam"** — What does this mean from the point of view of Advaita?

**Guru —** My son, the Advaitas first make a gross definition of sublatability — "The perfectly known of what is otherwise known is sublatability. This means that an object was previously known in another form under the influence of illusion, and later it was known in its true form. For example, in the knowledge that 'a rope is a snake', it is known first that it is a snake, and then it is known that it is a rope, and therefore the sublatability of the snake is proved.

**Disciple —** What is the defect in this characteristic that the Advaitas themselves refine it?

**Guru —** The main flaw here is that it is a 'perfect means' Look at the statement of the Advaitas — **"mithyātvakṣaṇikatvādinā vijñātasya prapañcasya satyatvasthāyitvādinā vijñātatvena siddhasādhanāt."**

Listen to the explanation of this — we, the dualistic, believe the universe to be true. Some Buddhists consider the universe to be 'momentary' If "perfect knowledge of what is otherwise known" is sublatability, then the world known by the Buddhists as 'transient' because of illusion will be hindered by knowledge of 'truth and permanence' in our dualistic theory. In our opinion, the universe is true, and therefore we are perfect to accept such sublatability. Advaitas, on the other hand, want to prove that the universe is false, not true. Therefore, in the pursuit of what is already perfect (the truth of the world), there is a 'perfect means' error.

**8. nyāyāmṛta  vākyam \- nāpi bādhakajñānaviṣayatvam, brahmaṇyativyāpteḥ | nāpi nāsti nāsīnna bhaviṣyatīti bodhyamānābhāvapratiyogitvena tadviṣayatvam | manmate hyāpaṇastharūpyasyaiva tatpratiyogitvāt |** 

**samīravāṇī \-** 

**Disciple —** Master, how is the eighth aspect of the explanation of falsity discussed in an Advaita manner? Why doesn't the aspect of "obstructive knowledge-subjectivity" apply to the characteristic of 'sublatability' here?

**Guru —** Calf, here the Advaitas discuss the characteristic of 'sublatability' in two ways. The Advaitas first consider that "Falsity is being the object of sublated knowledge. Their own trick there is this — **"nāpi bādhakajñānaviṣayatvam, brahmaṇyativyāpteḥ."**

This means — Sublating knowledge is knowledge of reality. When one sees a rope as a snake, and later has the obstructive knowledge that 'this is the rope', what is the subject of that knowledge? That subject is the **Locus**.  Even in the time of universal obstacle, 'Brahma' is the subject of obstacle knowledge. If 'being the object of obstacle knowledge' were false, then Brahman would also be false. So here is the **Over pervasive** error.

If what is objectified by the knowledge of the obstacle is false, the error of **Over pervasive** would fall on the Brahman. Because when the illusion of the world ceases, the obstacle knowledge (knowledge of reality) objectifies the **Locus** form of Brahman. If being the object of obstacle-knowledge were false, then Brahman would also be false.

**Disciple —** Let it be. So why not apply the second refinement — **"nāpi nāsti nāsīnna bhaviṣyatīti bodhyamānābhāvapratiyogitvena tadviṣayatvam."** — being the counter-positive of absence here?

**Guru —** Here is the mystery of the Advaitas' own doctrine. They say — **"manmate hyāpaṇastharūpyasyaiva tatpratiyogitvāt."**

**Disciple —** What does this mean? Advaita says here that it is according to Advaita, right?

**Teacher —** Yes. According to Advaita, 'Silver in shell' **illusion** arises in time, it is 'pratibhasika' When there is a prohibition of 'there is no silver', that prohibition is not of the one appearing in front, but of 'the real silver in the shop'

**Disciple —** If the prohibition is of the silver in the market, then the counter-positivity has gone there, not in the perceived silver\!

**Teacher —** Truth\! The **Under pervasive** error falls on what is perceived (pure silver), because the symptom does not go there. Because what is perceived in the Advaita view is 'inevitable', they mean that it cannot be directly 'competitive with the threefold prohibition'

**9. nyāyāmṛta  vākyam  \- nāpi jñānanivartyatvam | uttarajñānanivartyapūrvajñānādāviva sattve'pyupapatteḥ |** 

**samīravāṇī \-** 

**Disciple —** Master, the ninth aspect of falsity appears to be this — **"nor is it the reversibility of knowledge"**. The Advaitas, however, often say that 'Falsity is that which is removed by knowledge' Why is there a refutation here from the point of view of Advaita?

**Guru —** Calf, the Advaitas first discuss the gross characteristics. Their trick is this — **"uttarajñānanivartyapūrvajñānādāviva sattve'pyupapatteḥ |"** Listen to what this means. When subsequent knowledge is born in the world, the previous knowledge is destroyed. For example, after knowledge of 'this pot', knowledge of 'this cloth' ceases first.

**Disciple —** True, here prior knowledge has become knowledge-reversible. So is prior knowledge false?

**Teacher —** This is where the error lies. Even according to Advaita, knowledge is considered to be 'true' (for a while). If only "knowledge-reversibility" were false, then true prior knowledge would also be false. Even where the symptom should not go (in true knowledge), the going of the symptom is called **Over pervasive** error.

**Disciple —** Oh\! Then the renunciation of illusion is also the renunciation of knowledge. How do the Advaitas distinguish there?

**Guru —** The Advaitas say that — **"sattve'pyupapatteḥ"**. That is, 'knowledge-reversibility' is possible even in true objects (as in prior knowledge). Therefore, this characterization is not sufficient to accomplish the falsity of the universe. If we put it this way, then the symptom is **Over pervasive** fault-prone.

**10. nyāyāmṛta  vākyam \- nāpi svasamānādhikaraṇātyantābhāvapratiyogitvaṃ mithyātvam | avyāpyavṛttisaṃyogāderiva sattve'pyupapatteḥ |** 

**samīravāṇī \-** 

**Disciple —** Master, now let us consider the tenth aspect of falsehood. The Advaitas state the characteristics to establish their side — **"nor is it false to be competitive in the absence of the extreme of self-equality"**. What does this mean? What is the inconsistency here from the point of view of Advaita?

**Guru —** Calf, the Advaitas first discuss this characteristic. This characteristic means that — "the absolute absence which is in the locus of its own (object), which is its counter-positive or object of negation, is false" For example, where there is a rope, there is the extreme absence of a snake, so the snake is false.

**Disciple —** This characteristic seems to be correct. What fault do the Advaitas see here?

**Teacher —** They see the **Over pervasive** flaw here. Look at their line — **"Like the combination of incomprehensible instincts and so on sattve'pyupapatteḥ."**
The main theme here is 'contact' For example, there is a tree. There is a monkey sitting on the tip, so there is a 'monkey combination' But there is no monkey at the root of the same tree, so there is also the 'extreme absence of monkey union' In this case, 'monkey union' became a counter-positive or object of negation in its equivalent authority (tree).

**Disciple —** True. If this is the case, then 'monkey union' would be false. But even according to Advaita, union is accepted as 'true' (from a practical point of view).

**Teacher —** So \! Because union is a non-pervading substance, it remains with absence in its own authority. If only "competitiveness of the extreme absence of self-equality" were false, then this characteristic would go for true objects as well. This is the **Over pervasive** error. Here, however, truth is not to be said about illusion, and therefore the Advaitas believe that the characteristic is defective.

**11. nyāyāmṛta  vākyam \- nāpyavyāpyavṛttitvānāśrayasvasamānādhikaraṇātyantābhāvapratiyogi-**  
**tvam | āropitasya saṃyogasyātyantābhāvastu anāropitasya saṃyogasyātyantābhāvādanya eva vyāpyavṛttiriti vācyam | śuktirūpyādau tadabhāvādityuktatvād |** 

**samīravāṇī \-** 

**Disciple —** Master, the illustration of contact in the previous section demonstrated the error of **Over pervasive**. Now, how do the Advaitas consider the eleventh aspect to avoid that error?

**Guru —** Calf, the Advaitas say this while refining their characteristics — **"nāpyavyāpyavṛttitvānāśrayasvasamānādhikaraṇātyantābhāvapratiyogitvam |"**

This means that if a substance which is not 'non-pervading' is a counter-positive or object of negation in its authority, then it is false. In this way, the union does not become **Over pervasive**, because the union is non-pervading.

**Disciple —** Oh, this is a beautiful refinement\! What is the absence here again?

**The Guru —** Here the Advaitas themselves doubt that — **"But the extreme absence of the accused union is a pervasive instinct other than the extreme absence of the unattributed union.
**Disciple —** What is the difference between 'superimposed' and 'not superimposed' combinations here? Why do Advaitas divide like this?

**Guru —** Here the Advaitas demonstrate logical skill. In the previous section, we have seen that the 'tree-monkey union' is non-pervading, and therefore does not fall into the characteristic of falsity. To avoid that error, Advaitas make a new argument here:

1. **Natural/Real Contact:** The monkey contact in the tree is considered 'practical truth' (unattributed). He's in one part of the tree, not somewhere else. So he is **non-pervading**.

2. **Illusory/Superimposed Contact:** The contact that is assumed during the illusion (such as the blue contact in the sky in 'the sky is blue'), is only in the intellect. What is assumed (attributed) cannot be distinguished by country. Therefore, by making the imposed union appear everywhere, it is called **pervading**.

If the imputed union is pervading, then it again falls into the characteristic of **"self-equality of extreme absence competition"** (Falsity).

**Disciple —** Oh\! If the imputed coincidence is pervading, then it again falls into the characteristic of **"self-equal authority and extreme absence competition"** (Falsity). In this case, the characteristics of the Advaitas were successful, right?

**Teacher —** No, son\! This is where the Advaitas themselves find fault again. They say that if we thus distinguish, then **because it is said that there is no such thing in the beginning of purity and silver**. That is, our main goal is 'Shukti-Rajat' (silver in shell). Such 'absence of pervasive instinct' is not proved in Shukti-Rajat. In this case, the symptom does not go to the pure silver, so there is an **Under pervasive** error.

**Disciple —** So what does the main doctrine of the Advaitas say here?

**Guru —** The Advaitas, rejecting this solution, say — **"Because it is said that there is no such thing in the beginning of purity and silver."** This means that if such a subtle distinction is made, there would be an **Under pervasive** error of this characteristic. Because in illusory silver such a lack of pervasiveness is not achieved.

**Disciple —** Oh\! If this is the case, the Advaitas try to avoid one error, and another error (incomprehension) arises\!

**Teacher —** True. Therefore, the Advaitas say that all such characteristics are incomplete. They will state their own conclusion-characteristics in the future.

**Disciple** \- Your Majesty, earlier in the case of the idea of ​​falsity we have heard the \[11 definitions\] refutation of the eleven aspects. Now why does the author incarnate the twelfth aspect \[12th definition\]? What is the meaning of "not even knowledge and its functions"?

**Teacher** \- Calf, Advaitas talk about a new characteristic to support their side. According to them, whatever happens in **Ignorance or the Effect of Ignorance** is called Mithya. This is the characteristic presented by the phrase "no difference between knowledge and its actions.

**Disciple** \- Oh, then what is the attribution of this characteristic in our opinion (in the theory of duality)?

**Disciple** \- So if we just characterize it as '**ignorance**'?

**Teacher** \- This is our main dualistic theory. In our opinion, ignorance or ignorance is not indescribable, but it is the true form. **13. nyāyāmṛta  vākyam \-** tathāpi mithyāśabdo'nirvācyavacana iti pañcapādikārītyā sadasattvānadhikaraṇatvarūpānirvācyatvaṃ mithyātvam | tatprasiddhiśca khyātivāde vakṣyate || 1 ||

**Teacher** \- Calf, well asked. Now we shall see the principal characteristic of the Advaita doctrine. According to the Advaitas, the main meaning of 'mithya' is **indescribable nature\] The Panchapadika says that what cannot be defined as Real or Non-existent is 'indefinable'

**Disciple** \- **"sadasattvānadhikaraṇatvarūpam"** What does \[Being the locus of neither existence nor non-existence\] mean? What is the **Locus** \[Locus\] here?

This is the **Indescribable nature** \[Indescribable nature\]
